# Story directions

Eight complete story directions for the game, written on 2026-10-01 to choose from or mix. **None of this is in the
game.** No code, assets or levels changed; these are design documents only.

Every file has the same shape, so they're easy to compare:
- the logline, the world and its secret, the protagonist and the antagonist, the cast and the enemies;
- how Skyreach's tutorial plays in that story;
- the areas with level bands, and the plot in three acts with its ending;
- sample side quests;
- the loot fiction (who makes the guns, what rarity and the beams are, the chests, elements, five legendaries);
- how the game's own systems (respawn, falling, the HUD, the bestiary, levels) are explained in the fiction;
- the endgame, the signature moments, production notes, and why to pick it.

To skim, read the table below, then the *Cold open* and *Plot* of the ones that catch your eye.

## What every story keeps

The game that's built is canon, and all eight respect it:
- **Skyreach** ("Welcome to Skyreach"): the farmstead spawn and orchard, the village crossroads with the gun rack, the
  target meadow under the windmill, the creek, pond and footbridge, the waterfall off the rim, the spider grove, and
  the plateau with the ruined lookout facing the other islands. The tutorial's six steps play as built in every story;
  only the words change (Warden also hides the HUD until the range). Each story ends the tutorial at the lookout with
  its inciting incident.
- **The look**: rustic, harsh, lived-in frontier in stylized realism.
- **The loot**: guns built from parts, five rarities with colored beams, shared ammo pools, elements to come. Every
  story explains in its own fiction why guns come in parts and what the beams are.
- **The three chests**: the olive Supply Crate, the teal Strongbox and the granite-and-gold Reliquary with its orange
  runes and floating crystal. Every story gives the Reliquary a meaning, and in most it's central to the plot.
- **The creature concepts**: the Meadow Wolf (your pick), MossBack, Cliff Huntsman, Ember Tarantula and Sky Jumper.
  Every story uses all five as regional variants.
- **The gun designs**: the Kestrel bullpup and the Ranchhand shotgun, with Homestead, Regulator, Scrapjack, Zephyr,
  Farmhand, Coachman, Breacher and Thunderdrum as makers or legendary bases.
- **The bestiary's sections**: Creatures, Enemies, NPCs and Friends. Every cast table says which section each
  character belongs in.
- **Level 70**: each campaign ends near level 50 and the post-game climbs to the cap.

## My recommendation

**If I had to pick one: Revenant.**
- It's built like a looter shooter. Seven names on a list from the first hour, and each one is an island, a boss and a
  legendary. The player always knows what's next and why.
- It fits the art you've built. It's a western, and its supernatural layer (the Unpaid, Grave Sight) adds a whole
  enemy family without a second art kit.
- It gives the Reliquary its best meaning. It explains respawn and falling naturally, and it hands you an endgame (the
  Ledger's bounties) without contortions.
- Its twist (the patron is the villain) and its heart (the Deacon) are strong, and cheap to deliver: a few scenes,
  good lines, the bestiary in Sexton's hand.

**Runner-up: Below.** It has the best endgame of the eight: dives scale forever, and the fiction explains every rule
(the lantern is the clock, depth is the difficulty, you keep only what you bring back up). It reuses the most of the
island kit, since the Fallfield is your own buildings wrecked on the clouds, and its emotional core is strong. Pick it
over Revenant if the long-term loop matters more to you than the campaign.

The rest: **The Lodestone War** is the safest. **Leviathan** is the most original, and the most expensive.
**Boomtown** is the most loot-first. **Skyfarers** gives Skyreach the best twist. **Warden** explains the most
systems. **The Long Dusk** gets the most visual variety for the least art.

### Mixes that work

- **Revenant with Below's endgame.** The Gravewind carries the dead *down* into the clouds instead of out to a far
  shore, Sexton's Toll Gate is at the bottom, and the endgame is dives into the land of the dead after the souls who
  escaped.
- **The Lodestone War with Below's endgame.** The Company's Deep Veins become full dives under the islands.
- **Any story with Boomtown's Hettie.** A legendary gunsmith NPC whose Masterworks are side quests, with crafting at
  the hub.
- **Any story with Warden's HUD boot-up.** The HUD switches on piece by piece in the tutorial.

## Ideas that work in any of them

- **Re-skin the tutorial.** The six steps stay as built; only their prompt text changes, and the lookout gets the
  inciting incident.
- **A hub that changes.** Every story damages, darkens or transforms Skyreach at the end of Act II (burned, sunk, dusk,
  occupied, sailing). Those can be swappable versions of the same level.
- **The spider concepts by region:** the Meadow Wolf on meadows, the MossBack in the damp, the Cliff Huntsman on
  cliffs, the Ember Tarantula in heat, the Sky Jumper up high.
- **The gun concepts as makers.** The unused AR designs (Homestead, Regulator, Scrapjack, Zephyr) make good legendary
  bases, as `Art/Backlog/README.md` already suggests.
- **A Lift element** (orange: targets float, and near an edge, drift away) suits any floating world.
- **The bestiary as an in-world book** written by a character: Odessa's Settling Book, Sexton's Ledger, Dr. Quist's
  field guide, the Fathom Ledger, Bess's fables, Mr. Pockets' Opinions. The Description and Notes fields already carry
  that voice.
- **One line of flavor text on every legendary**: the cheapest storytelling in the genre.
- **A wildcard structure: the seven-day loop.** Skyreach falls into the clouds at sundown on the seventh day, and you
  wake on the first with your loot and your memories, to work out why. It concentrates content on a few islands
  (cheap), but needs NPC schedules and state that resets (expensive). It would graft onto Below or Warden.

## What any of them needs built

Beyond what exists today (the shooter, guns from parts, loot, the bestiary, the spider, progression and the tutorial
island), every direction needs:
- **Human enemies with guns**: an AI that takes cover and shoots back. This is the biggest missing piece, since every
  story's main enemy faction is people.
- **A quest and dialogue layer**: objectives, NPC lines, and story state saved with the player's progress.
- **Vendors and a currency.**
- **Travel between islands**: a skiff, airship or lift between levels. Each island as its own level suits loading and
  the per-area performance budget.
- **Pistols, SMGs and snipers** (the ammo types exist), **chests** (the models exist) and **elements**.
- **Boss fights with phases.**

Each story's production notes list what it adds on top.

## After you pick

1. Turn the chosen story (and any mix) into a canon story bible, `Docs/Story.md`, and point `CLAUDE.md` at it.
2. Re-skin the tutorial prompts and the first bestiary pages in the story's voice.
3. Plan the first new island as a layout file, like `Art/Levels/TutorialIsland/layout.json`.
4. Build the first human enemy faction and the first boss.
5. Write the first quest chain on Skyreach.
