# 2. Revenant

> *Seven outlaws killed you. Something sent you back to bring them in.*

The Dunne Gang shot Ellis Ransom on Skyreach's lookout, robbed the island's saint and rode away. A week later Ellis
claws out of a grave in the family orchard, with a soft-spoken stranger holding their soul in pawn until all seven are
brought down. No living gun can kill what the gang has become.

## Cold open

Dusk on the lookout. Seven riders, a smashed Reliquary and a burning orange ember in a gloved hand. Ellis Ransom,
youngest of the gang, steps between the Deacon and the stairs: *"Not here. Not my home."* The Deacon's face, sad and
fond. *"I'm sorry, kid."* One shot, and the sky turns over.

A week later: birdsong, a bright blue afternoon, bees in the orchard. A hand breaks through the dirt of a fresh grave.

## The world

- In the Reaches the dead are **given to the wind**. Farm families bury their dead for seven days to say goodbye, then
  lay them on a burial board at the island's edge, and at dusk the **Gravewind**, a cold wind that blows only then, carries their souls past the edge of the sky to **the Far
  Shore**.
- Every island has a **saint**: its first settler, laid in a granite **Reliquary** banded in gold, whose soul stays
  behind as an **ember** of orange light floating over the lid. The saints are lighthouses. Their light shows the dead
  the way to the Far Shore. Nobody touches a Reliquary, ever.
- An island that loses its saint loses its way: its dead stop crossing. They linger as the **Unpaid**, cold, hungry
  and angry, and the living there sicken and go strange.
- Six months ago the **Dunne Gang**, eight outlaws led by a defrocked preacher, was hired by a stranger who paid in old
  gold to rob seven Reliquaries on seven islands and take the embers. Seven of them ended up carrying one apiece (Ellis
  never would; something about the embers felt wrong), and each ember changed its carrier. Six of them became **the
  Hollow**: a saint's power soured inside a living sinner. (The Deacon fought his ember, and it's killing him instead.)
  No living hand can kill a Hollow. It takes someone already dead.
- The seventh job was Skyreach, Ellis's home. Ellis balked, and the Deacon shot them.

## Protagonist: Ellis Ransom

Ellis grew up on the Ransom farm on Skyreach and left at sixteen to ride with the Deacon, a charismatic outlaw-preacher
who took in strays and called them family. Ten years of stagecoach jobs and bank jobs later, Ellis was the youngest and
the best shot in the gang, and the one the Deacon loved most.

Now Ellis is a **Revenant**: cold to the touch, no heartbeat, wounds that close, and eyes that see souls. Ellis wants
three things, in the order they admit them: revenge, rest, and to make right what they helped do to Skyreach's dead.
The arc runs from vengeance to atonement. The story's real question isn't whether Ellis can kill seven Hollow. It's
whether Ellis can forgive the one who shot them, and themself.

> *"I'm not here to kill you, Ned. You're already dead. I'm here to collect."*

## Antagonist: Mister Sexton

He is tall and patient and always polite, in an undertaker's coat and a stovepipe hat, his face in shadow even at
noon. He smells like rain and keeps a ledger. He sits on the lookout railing, legs crossed, and offers Ellis a deal:
bring back the seven embers and their soul will be released to rest. Fail, and Ellis rides the Gravewind forever with
no shore at the end.

What he is: before there were saints, the dead needed a **ferryman** to cross, and every soul paid him. When the first
settlers became saints and lit the way, the dead stopped needing him. He has been starving at the edge of the sky ever
since. He hired the gang. Now he needs a Revenant to pry the embers back out of the Hollow so he can snuff them at the
**Toll Gate**. With every saint dark, every soul in the Reaches would have to pay his toll again, and the ones who
can't pay would serve him.

> *"Everyone pays the ferryman, friend. I'm only asking for what's owed."*

### The Deacon, Ambrose Dunne

The gang's leader and Ellis's mentor, almost a father. A preacher thrown out of his church for saying heaven was a
toll road, he kept Skyreach's ember for himself. Unlike the others he didn't go Hollow. He fought it, and it's killing
him instead. He shot Ellis because Ellis drew on him first and he was faster. There was no secret mercy in it, and he
has been sick about it every day since. He's the last name on the list and the hardest.

> *"I've buried a lot of people, kid. You're the only one who ever came back to ask me why."*

## Enemies and creatures

- **The Unpaid**: the restless dead of the robbed islands, pale shapes in the clothes they died in, with a cold orange
  coal where the heart should be. They phase through walls and shriek. Ellis sees them clearly; the living barely do.
- **Each outlaw's following**, the living, slowly going Hollow: Ned's card sharps and bouncers, Ira's dust riders,
  Constance's flock of zealots, Barrels's furnace men, Lena's spotters, Tobias's quarry crew. One rig set, seven
  costumes.
- **Bounty hunters**: there's a price on the walking corpse, and the living come to claim it in Act II.
- **The Mourners**: Sexton's servants from the end of Act II, murders of crows that fold into figures in black.
- **Creatures**, drawn to the dead:
  - Meadow Wolf spiders nest in graveyards.
  - MossBacks in Saint Agnes Mire.
  - Ember Tarantulas in Furnace Hollow's slag.
  - Cliff Huntsmen on the Needles.
  - Sky Jumpers in the *Gilded Lily*'s rigging.
  - **Bone Hounds** (coyotes that ate from the burial boards) and **Carrion Gulls**.

## Skyreach in this story

1. **Move.** Ellis claws out of the grave in the orchard behind the farmstead. Grandma Delia drops her basket.
2. **Follow the road to the village.** People run, doors slam. Father Aldana raises a cross with shaking hands.
3. **The gun rack.** The townsfolk hung Ellis's own rifle on the rack in the square as a memorial, with a wreath.
   Ellis takes it back.
4. **The dummies.** Tilly wants to know whether a dead hand can still shoot. It can.
5. **The spiders.** The spiders in the woods have gone strange, and they come for Ellis like moths to a lamp.
6. **The loadout.** Ellis checks the rifle. Somebody cleaned it for the memorial, which Ellis never once did.
7. **The lookout.** Where Ellis died. The boards are still stained. Mister Sexton sits on the railing in the sunset with
   a crow on his shoulder: *"Shall we talk business?"*

## Plot

### Act I: Dust to Dust (levels 1–16)

1. The cold open, the grave, the tutorial and the deal on the lookout. Sexton's terms: bring each ember to the lookout
   at the new moon. Each one paid returns a little of Ellis's life: warmth, color, a heartbeat for a minute.
2. **The *Gilded Lily*.** Ned Purcell runs a floating casino where the house always wins. The house loses. His ember
   goes to Sexton, and Ellis feels the sun on their skin for the first time since dying.
3. **Dustwater Mesa.** Ira Gale, inside his storm. Dying, he coughs: *"He paid us in old gold, Ellis. Gold that'd been
   under the clouds."*
4. Ranger Ruth Calder ambushes Ellis outside Dustwater: dead or not, Ellis rode with the Dunne Gang. Then the Unpaid
   pour out of the dust and the two of them fight back to back. It's an uneasy truce.

### Act II: The Unpaid (levels 16–40)

5. **Saint Agnes Mire.** Sister Constance raises the dead because she thinks she's helping them, since they can't
   cross. Before the end she tells Ellis: *"The saints don't hold souls back, child. They light the way. Put them out
   and the dead can't find the shore. And somebody's waiting at the gate to sell them directions."*
6. **Furnace Hollow.** Barrels Kessler, burning from the inside out. His foundry is full of the Unpaid of the island
   he robbed.
7. **The Needles.** Lena Okoro describes the man who hired them: tall, polite, a stovepipe hat.
8. Hob confesses. He was a Revenant once, eighty years ago. He paid his debt in full, and Sexton made him a crow.
   *"Every Revenant he makes ends up with feathers, kid. Ask me how I know."*
9. **Stoneweight Quarry.** Tobias Grant doesn't want to fight; he offers his ember if Ellis lets his crew walk. Ellis
   can accept, but Sexton's Mourners arrive and force the fight anyway.
10. **The new moon.** Ellis comes to the lookout with the sixth ember and refuses to hand it over. Sexton stops being
    polite. He takes it, and Ellis dies again and wakes in the orchard grave, greyer and colder than before. Sexton
    now holds six saints. He needs one more: Skyreach's, in the Deacon's pocket.

### Act III: The Gravewind (levels 40–50)

11. Ellis, Ruth, Tilly and Hob make a plan: reach the Deacon before Sexton does, keep Skyreach's ember, take the other
    six back at the Toll Gate.
12. **Gravesend.** The necropolis, crawling with the Unpaid. The Deacon is dying in a mausoleum, holding Skyreach's
    ember to his chest. Ellis and the Deacon finally talk: no excuses, no secret reasons, just a coward's confession
    and a kid who came home. The Mourners attack, and for one fight the Deacon stands beside Ellis again.
13. **The Hollow Deacon.** He dies, and Sexton climbs into his body. Ellis has to fight their mentor's corpse while
    his own voice keeps apologizing. When it's over, Skyreach's ember is in Ellis's hand.
14. **The Toll Gate.** At dusk Ellis steps off the lookout and rides the Gravewind to the edge of the sky: a pier of
    grey wood, a lantern, and every unpaid soul in the Reaches standing in line. Sexton at the gate is a towering
    ferryman made of black cloth and crows, with a long rifle called Toll. Hob flies at his face.

### Ending

The six embers spill free and Ellis carries them, and Skyreach's, home: seven saints relit, seven islands whose dead
can finally cross. The Gravewind blows, and thousands of souls walk past Ellis to the Far Shore. The Deacon is among
them, and he tips his hat. Ellis's debt is paid. They could go too.

They don't. Sexton's ledger, now in Ellis's hand, is full of souls who slipped away while the saints were dark. *"Not
yet."* Hob settles on their shoulder. Grandma Delia sets a plate.

## Side quests (samples)

- **Unfinished Business.** An Unpaid farmer won't leave until someone finishes plowing his last field. Plow it, under
  fire.
- **Burial Board.** A town lost its undertaker. Escort its dead to the Gravewind while the Bone Hounds circle.
- **Ruth's Brother.** Ruth's brother rode with a rival gang and died Hollow. She wants him found and put down by the
  only hand that can.
- **The House Always Wins.** A card game with the dead on the wreck of the *Gilded Lily*. The pot is a legendary; the
  stakes are your next life.

## Loot in this world

- **Who makes the guns.** Frontier makers: Coachman and Breacher shotguns, Regulator rifles, Homestead farm guns,
  Kestrel carbines. Out here, guns come off the dead and get rebuilt by whoever's handy, so every gun is a patchwork
  of other guns' parts. The Hollow's guns are **soul-forged irons**: weapons that killed so much that the dead stayed
  in them.
- **Rarity and the beams.** Everything a dead hand held keeps a little of its owner, and Ellis sees it burn. The loot
  beam is that leftover soul-light, and its color is how much of the owner's will stayed in the gun.
- **Chests.** Supply Crates are Ranger caches. Strongboxes are safes from the gang's old bank jobs. Reliquaries are the
  saints themselves: each opens for Ellis the moment its ember is pried out of a Hollow, as the saint's thanks,
  before the ember has to be handed on.
- **Elements (when they come).** **Ember** (fire), **Grave** (rot over time, with bonus damage against the Hollow),
  **Wind** (knockback toward edges) and **Spirit** (the only damage that hurts the Unpaid; passes through thin cover).

## Endgame (levels 50–70)

- **The Ledger.** Repeatable bounties on souls who escaped while the saints were dark: named Hollow outlaws with
  rolled traits, each with a chance at a soul-forged legendary. Bounty tiers set the danger.
- **The Gravesend Deeps.** Catacombs under the necropolis, assembled from a room kit, deeper and deadlier floor by
  floor.
- **The Midnight Stage.** Escort a ghostly stagecoach of the dead across islands to the Gravewind while the Unpaid try
  to drag its passengers off.
- **Hanging Moon.** A recurring night on Skyreach when the dead walk the orchard. Hold the graves until dawn.

## Production notes

- **Reuses:** the western art kit as it is; graves, crosses and burial boards are cheap props; the Reliquary is the
  story's centerpiece.
- **New:** ghost enemies (translucent, phasing: emissive and dithered, priced on Medium before anything else); a Grave
  Sight vision mode (a post-process pass, measured); night lighting for some areas; eight bespoke boss fights (the seven and
  Sexton), the main cost.
- **Risk:** medium. The boss roster is big, but each boss anchors one area and one set of enemies, so the work comes in
  clean pieces.

## Why pick it, and what to watch

Pick it for the crispest structure of the eight: seven names on a list from the first hour, each one a place, a boss and
a legendary. It's a western that fits the art already built, with a supernatural layer that triples the enemy
variety without a second art kit, and an endgame (the Ledger) the story hands you directly. Watch out: it's darker than
the sunny meadow suggests (that contrast is the hook, so own it). Eight bosses need eight distinct fights. And "the
patron is the villain" has to be seeded fairly, through Hob, Constance and Lena, so the turn feels earned.
