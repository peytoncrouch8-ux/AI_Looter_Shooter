# Revenant

> *Seven outlaws killed you. Something sent you back to bring them in.*

On Teropa, in the frontier country called the Reaches, the Dunne Gang shot Ellis Ransom on the lookout above the family farm on Ransom's Rest. They shot Ellis's father on the path below, robbed the town's saint and flew off in their skiff. A week later Ellis claws out of a grave in the family plot. A soft-spoken stranger holds their soul in pawn until all seven outlaws are brought down. No living gun can kill what the gang has become.

This is the game's story: `Docs/Story/2-Revenant.md` from the stories branch, revised on 2026-10-01 with your three changes and a way off the tutorial island, then the same day with your answers to the six questions of step 1, and then with your step 2 feedback: the story takes place on Teropa, an earth-like planet, and Ransom's Rest is solid ground, not an island. Last came your answers to the six questions that feedback raised: travel between regions is by train, and whether the *Gilded Lily* stays an airship waits until Ransom's Rest is complete (see *Decisions*). It lives in the repo as `Docs/Story.md`, the single canon, and `CLAUDE.md` points to it. The first area has its own document, `Docs/Areas/RansomsRest.md` (*Ransom's Rest: the first area*). ## Cold open

It follows straight on from the first skiff ride off the tutorial island (see *Leaving the tutorial island*), and plays the same for players who skip the tutorial. It plays once per session; later trips to and from Skyreach are plain transitions with no cutscene. The title REVENANT rises through the white of the cloud bank, and the white thins.

1. **Seven days ago.** The player stands on a skiff's deck in the same spot, looking the same way, still free to look around. But it's a different skiff: the Dunne Gang's, dark-hulled, gliding out of a bank of evening cloud in the west, low over the plains and up Gravewind Canyon toward Ransom's Rest at sunset. Seven figures stand on deck in silhouette. The Deacon, beside Ellis: *“Home, kid.”* Caption: *Ransom's Rest. Seven days ago.* Ellis told them where to moor: in a cleft of the canyon under the cliffs of Ransom's Point, the one place on Ransom's Rest nobody in town can see. Fade.
2. **Dusk on Ransom's Point**, the lookout above the Ransom farm, seen through Ellis's eyes. The chapel bell clangs across the Rest. The gang is back from the chapel, and the Deacon holds Saint Ada's ember, a burning orange light, in a gloved hand. Ellis steps between him and the cliff stairs down to the skiff: *“Put her back, Deacon. Not here. Not my home.”* Abel Ransom, Ellis's father and the Keeper of Saint Ada, runs up the bluff path with a lantern and a shotgun: *“El!”* Lucky Ned fires. Abel falls, and his lantern rolls off the path into the dark. Ellis draws on the Deacon. The Deacon is faster, and his face is sad and fond: *“I'm sorry, kid.”* The sky turns over. Far along the railing, a tall man in a stovepipe hat sits with his legs crossed, watching. Black.
3. **Seven days later.** Birdsong, bees, a golden late-summer afternoon. A hand breaks through the dirt of a fresh grave in the Ransom family plot; this is the player's first input. The headboard reads *ELLIS RANSOM. CAME HOME AT THE LAST.* The one beside it reads *ABEL RANSOM. KEEPER OF SAINT ADA. HE HELD THE DOOR.* Its mound is whole, but frost lies on it in the afternoon sun, and the flowers on it have gone black. A one-eyed crow lands on Ellis's headboard: *“Morning, sunshine. Most folks who get up do it after dark.”*

It is staged cheaply on purpose: a camera moved by code, captions, sound and one muzzle flash. The gang are flat black silhouettes (the UE mannequin, posed, with no costumes). The man on the railing is Sexton's seated model drawn in black. The gang's skiff is the tutorial's skiff model in dark paint, and the evening cloud is the same cloud-bank card as the tutorial's.

## The world

- **Teropa** (new). The story takes place on Teropa, an earth-like world with one sun, one moon, seasons, seas and mountain ranges, and solid ground under every town. All of it happens in **the Reaches**, the western frontier of Teropa's great continent, where settlement ends: farm valleys and river gorges, mesas, swamp country, smelter towns and quarries. Its western edge is **the Rim**, a long rimrock escarpment over deep canyons, which the Rim Rangers ride. Past it the plains run empty to the sunset: nobody settles the land the dead cross. The railroad runs to the Rim and no farther.
- **Ransom's Rest** (new). A high valley of late-summer farmland on the Rim. Ridges close it on the north, east and south: Larkspur Ridge, the East Ridge and the Hogback. On the west it ends at a 70 m drop into Gravewind Canyon. Past the canyon the empty plains run on to the sunset, so from the valley you can watch the sun go all the way down. The ground is broken: cliff bands at the ridges' feet, rock outcrops and a lone butte in the fields, a dry wash running to the Rim, and the ring of cliffs where the Sink fell in. The place and its town share the name, and locals call it “the Rest”.
- In the Reaches the dead are **given to the wind**. Farm families bury their dead for seven days to say goodbye, with their guns beside them (“buried with their irons”). Then the keeper lays them on a **burial board** on high ground facing west and sits the night beside them by lantern light. At dusk the **Gravewind**, a cold wind that blows only then, runs down off the high ground, lifts their souls and carries them west into the sunset, down **the Sundown Road** (new), toward **the Far Shore**. At dawn the keeper carries the body home to its grave for good, under its headboard. On Ransom's Rest the burial boards stand on Gravewind Point, a promontory of the Rim, where the wind pours off the Rim and out over the canyon at dusk.
- **The Far Shore is on no map** (new). The Sundown Road leaves the world where the sun goes down and ends at a grey pier on a sea of fog, and the shore lies across the fog. The crossing is long: a soul follows its saint's light for years before it gets there.
- Every town has a **saint**: its first settler, laid in a granite **Reliquary** banded in gold. The saint's soul stays behind as an **ember** of orange light floating over the lid. The saints are lighthouses. Their light shines down the Sundown Road and lays a way across the fog, and a town's dead steer by it all the way to the Far Shore. Nobody touches a Reliquary, ever.
- **The old fare** (new). Before there were saints, the dead crossed the fog by ferry and paid the ferryman, and families laid a gold coin on each eye of their dead for the fare. When the first settlers became saints and lit the way, the dead stopped needing a ferry, and the custom died out. The old coins still turn up in old graves, and folks call them **grave gold**. The pier at the end of the road is still called **the Toll Gate**.
- **Keepers** (new). Every saint has a keeper family. They tend the Reliquary, walk the dead to the burial boards by lantern light and sit up with them until dawn. A keeper's lantern is lit from the saint, and its flame leans toward a saint's light wherever that light is carried. On Ransom's Rest the keepers are the Ransoms. A keeper doesn't lie still while his saint is dark.
- **The Unpaid.** When a saint goes dark, every soul of its town still on the Sundown Road loses the light and drifts home to its own grave. Those are the Unpaid: cold, hungry and angry, and the living near them sicken and go strange. Ransom's Rest went dark seven days ago, and years of its dead have come home.
- **Dead hands.** No living gun can kill a Hollow, but any gun in a dead hand hurts the dead. The Unpaid know it, and they come for Ellis on sight.
- **The Dunne Gang.** Six months ago the gang, eight outlaws led by a defrocked preacher, was hired by a stranger who paid in grave gold. The job was to rob seven Reliquaries in seven towns across the Reaches and take the embers. In every town they shot the keeper first and smashed his lantern, because a keeper's lantern can find an ember. Seven of the gang ended up carrying one apiece. Ellis never would: something about the embers felt wrong. Each ember changed its carrier. Six of them became **the Hollow**, a saint's power soured inside a living sinner. The Deacon fought his ember, and it's killing him instead. No living hand can kill a Hollow. It takes someone already dead.
- **The seventh job** was Ransom's Rest, Ellis's home. Ellis guided the gang in: out of a bank of evening cloud in the west, low over the plains and up Gravewind Canyon, to moor under the cliffs of Ransom's Point, the one place nobody in town can see. Ellis balked at the end. Abel came running, Ned shot him, and the Deacon shot Ellis.
- **What a lantern can't find** (new). A lantern leans toward the nearest saint's light burning in the world. Two things hide an ember from it. An ember paid to Sexton goes into his ledger, past any lantern's reach. And a Hollow who fights his ember holds its light down; only the Deacon fights his.
- **Getting around** (new). The railroad carries people, freight and Ellis between the regions of the Reaches. Every region has a station: a depot with a platform. Undertakers keep black hearse cars for coffins, hitched behind the passenger car. Some are bound for **Gravesend**, the great necropolis on the Rim's highest cliffs, where the Gravewind blows hardest and where towns with no keeper send their dead. On Ransom's Rest the line comes in through Stage Gap, a rail cut through a saddle in the East Ridge, from the wooded valley beyond, and the depot stands at the east end of town by the undertaker's yard. Skiffs, small gas-bag airships, are rare. The Dunne Gang's is an outlaw's airship, bought with Sexton's grave gold, and it comes in where no depot sees it.

*Outside the story.* Teropa has no floating islands. Skyreach, the tutorial and practice island, stays one, and it stays outside the story: nothing in the story explains it, and the story never names it. The tutorial's last image is a skiff sailing into a cloud bank. It cuts to the story's first: the gang's skiff coming out of the evening cloud west of Ransom's Rest.

## Protagonist: Ellis Ransom

Ellis grew up on the Ransom farm on Ransom's Rest, in the family that kept Saint Ada. At sixteen Ellis ran from that duty to ride with the Deacon, a charismatic outlaw-preacher who took in strays and called them family. Ten years of stagecoach and bank jobs later, Ellis was the youngest and the best shot in the gang, and the one the Deacon loved most.

Now Ellis is a **Revenant**: cold to the touch, no heartbeat, wounds that close, and eyes that see souls. Ellis wants three things, in the order they admit them: revenge, rest, and to make right what they helped do to the dead of Ransom's Rest, starting with Pa. The arc runs from vengeance to atonement. The story's real question isn't whether Ellis can kill seven Hollow. It's whether Ellis can forgive the one who shot them, and themself.

> *“I'm not here to kill you, Ned. You're already dead. I'm here to collect.”*

## Antagonist: Mister Sexton

He is tall and patient and always polite, in an undertaker's coat and a stovepipe hat, his face in shadow even at noon. He smells like rain and keeps a ledger. He sits on the railing of Ransom's Point with his legs crossed and offers Ellis a deal: bring back the seven embers and their soul will be released to rest. Fail, and Ellis rides the Gravewind forever with no shore at the end. He adds a sweetener: *“And your father crosses. I'll see to his fare myself.”*

He was on that railing the night Ellis died. Asked, he doesn't deny it: *“I attend every death, friend. It's my trade.”* That's true, and it isn't the whole truth.

What he is: before there were saints, the dead crossed the sea of fog at the end of the Sundown Road on his ferry, and each paid the **ferryman** with a gold coin on each eye. When the saints' light laid a way across, nobody needed the ferry. He has been starving at the **Toll Gate**, the pier at the road's end, ever since. He hired the gang. Now he needs a Revenant to pry the embers back out of the Hollow so he can snuff them at the Toll Gate. With every saint dark, every soul in the Reaches would have to pay his toll again, and the ones who can't pay would serve him.

> *“Everyone pays the ferryman, friend. I'm only asking for what's owed.”*

### The Deacon, Ambrose Dunne

The gang's leader and Ellis's mentor, almost a father. He was a preacher thrown out of his church for saying heaven was a toll road, and he kept the Rest's ember, Saint Ada's, for himself. Unlike the others he didn't go Hollow. He fought it, and it's killing him instead; that fight is also why no lantern can find Saint Ada. He shot Ellis because Ellis drew on him first and he was faster. There was no secret mercy in it, and he has been sick about it every day since. He's the last name on the list and the hardest.

> *“I've buried a lot of people, kid. You're the only one who ever came back to ask me why.”*

## Enemies and creatures

- **The Unpaid**: the restless dead of the robbed towns. They are pale shapes in the clothes they died in, fading below the waist into a trailing shroud, with a dull red coal where the heart should be. They drift, phase through walls and shriek. Ellis sees them clearly; the living barely do.
- **Ranks**: some creatures and dead have fed longer on the dark. Ellis sees it as soul-light: the Restless glow blue, the Gravebound purple, the Soulfed and the named horrors orange. An Unpaid's coal burns in the same color. Brighter souls hit harder and carry better iron (see *Enemy ranks and legendary drops*).
- **Each outlaw's following**, the living, slowly going Hollow: Ned's card sharps and bouncers, Ira's dust riders, Constance's flock of zealots, Barrels's furnace men, Lena's spotters, Tobias's quarry crew. One rig set, seven costumes.
- **Bounty hunters**: there's a price on the walking corpse, and the living come to claim it in Act II.
- **The Mourners**: Sexton's servants from the end of Act II, murders of crows that fold into figures in black.

  - Meadow Wolf spiders nest in graveyards and hoard anything a saint has touched: the Rest's chapel yard, the bluff and the Sink. The Gravemother is the legendary spider of Ransom's Rest, with a side mission of her own.

## Leaving the tutorial island (new)

**Where.** A timber jetty on the plateau's south-east rim, a few metres past the ruined lookout. Its root sits about 6 m inland of the rim (near layout X −6550, Y 6450 cm; the exact point is taken from the rim when it is placed), and it reaches about 16 m out over the drop. At its root stand a bell post and a chalk slate: *“Skiff departs when you're ready.”* A few distant islands (new, cheap backdrop meshes, around Skyreach only) hang in the sky beyond it; today the tutorial island has only its painted cloud dome.

**The vessel.** A packet skiff: a 9 m flat-bottomed plank hull under a patched canvas gas-bag, with a bow lantern and mooring ropes. It is moored there from the start, so it can be seen from the plateau path, but its gangplank is up.

**How it plays.**
1. The tutorial keeps its six steps. When they are done, or skipped, the gangplank lowers and the bell rings.
2. A new mission, “Board the skiff”, appears with a waypoint on the jetty: *“Your skiff is in. Board it at the jetty past the lookout when you're ready to leave Skyreach.”* It is its own mission, not a seventh tutorial step, so it also shows for players who skipped the tutorial and for sessions that finished it before this change.
3. Holding Interact at the gangplank (the same one-second hold as equipping) opens the station board, the LooterUI panel every depot uses later. On the first cast-off it shows one destination and a confirm: *“Leave Skyreach? Your story begins. You can come back to practice any time.”* The choices are Cast off and Not yet.
4. Cast off: the player stands on deck with look-only control. The skiff slips its ropes and drifts out for about 12 seconds while Skyreach falls behind. Then it sails into a cloud bank and the screen goes white.
5. Behind the white, the session saves (its map becomes Ransom's Rest, it records that the player has cast off, and it keeps Skyreach's world state and the inventory) and Ransom's Rest loads. The title card rises through the white, and the cold open begins.

**How it ties into the story: the cloud match cut.** When the white thins, the player is standing on another skiff's deck, in the same spot and looking the same way. It is the Dunne Gang's skiff coming out of a bank of evening cloud west of Ransom's Rest seven days ago, and the player is Ellis. That is how Ellis came home. The player's last image of the tutorial (a skiff going into a cloud) becomes the story's first image (a skiff coming out of one), and Skyreach is never named or explained. On Teropa the cloud is only weather, so the cut needs no sky islands: the gang's skiff comes out over the plains and flies up Gravewind Canyon to the Rest.
- The guns from the tutorial come along into the story: whatever the player carries comes out of the grave with Ellis, because the Reaches bury their dead with their irons. The story doesn't say where the guns came from. With “Skip the tutorial”, Ellis starts with a Common Bullpup in the coffin.
- The skiff is not the story's way to travel; the railroad is (see *Getting around*). The only skiff the story shows is the gang's, the same model in dark paint, so the skiff has two paints: packet for the tutorial and dark for the gang.
- Hob is not on the tutorial skiff. He first appears at the grave.

**Back to Skyreach for practice.** The first cast-off plays the cloud match cut and the cold open. After that, Skyreach stays reachable as a practice island:
- Every station board (at the depot on Ransom's Rest, and at every later station) lists “Skyreach (practice)” as a destination at any time. Choosing it is a plain transition (a fade, no cutscene) to Skyreach's jetty.
- Skyreach's jetty board lists every opened area, and returns the player to that area's station by the same plain transition. The plain transition both ways is a default you can change.
- Skyreach stays outside the story: no story characters, lines or plot. Hob doesn't come along or comment there, and the story never names it.
- Its creatures give 0 XP. By default they drop only ammo (no guns) on return visits, so it stays practice and not a loot farm. This is a default you can change. A return visit means any visit after the first cast-off.
- The session save keeps each map's world state, Skyreach's included.
- New sessions still offer “Skip the tutorial”, which goes straight to the cold open with a Common Bullpup in the coffin. It counts as the first cast-off, so “Skyreach (practice)” is on the station boards for these players too. The tutorial gives no XP from now on, so new sessions arrive on Ransom's Rest at level 1; older sessions keep what they already earned.

## Ransom's Rest, the first area (summary)

**The place** (the step 2 map, on solid ground):
- A high farm valley on the Rim, about 309 × 205 m (roughly 1.5× the tutorial island), in one level.
- Ridges with cliff bands close it on the north, east and south. On the west the Rim drops 70 m into Gravewind Canyon.
- The gang's hidden mooring is a ledge in a cleft of the canyon under Ransom's Point. The burial boards stand on Gravewind Point over the canyon. Mill Creek falls 14 m into a gorge through the Hogback. The railroad comes in through Stage Gap, a rail cut through a saddle in the East Ridge, from the wooded valley beyond, and the depot and its platform stand at the east end of town by the undertaker's yard.
- Cliffs and obstacles break up the open ground: Coffin Rock (a lone butte), the Dry Wash, rock outcrops such as the Three Widows and the Stone Teeth, the Tumble's rockfall, Widow's Pines, ruins, boulders, stone walls and fences. Outside the zones no open stretch is wider than about 19 m, except the crossroads at the keeper's grave, kept clear on purpose.

The first area and the hub, in order (the full plan is `Docs/Areas/RansomsRest.md`):

2. **Shall We Talk Business?** Up the bluff to Ransom's Point, the lookout where Ellis died, about 15 minutes in. The first ranked enemy, a blue Restless spider, nests by the lookout. Sexton's deal comes with the Ledger of seven names. The gang scattered, so Ellis asks where to start. Sexton: *“Ask a keeper. Their lanterns lean toward a saint's light.”* *“The keeper's dead.”* *“So are you, friend.”*
3. **Cold Welcome.** Into town. The Unpaid come for the corpse at the gate. Tilly, at her window, says Pa's lantern wasn't on him when she laid him out.
4. **Hallowed Ground.** The chapel yard, the bell and the smashed Reliquary. Aldana says the gang shot the keeper first in every town and smashed his lantern. Abel's fell in the dark, whole, and spiders hoard what a saint has touched: look in the Sink.
5. **The Keeper's Lantern.** Down into the Sink for it.
6. **The Gravewind.** At dusk Ellis carries the lantern to the burial boards. Pa is the boss. Afterwards he lights the lantern from the ghost light he carries, and its flame leans north-east, over the ridges, toward Ned.
7. **The Lantern Leans.** Delia hands Heirloom, Pa's shotgun, out through the door. Tilly's hearse car waits at the depot, hitched to the train, and the station board lists the *Gilded Lily*. (The train stands at the platform from the start, cold and shut; from this mission it has steam up and the hearse car's door is open.)

## Campaign gating (new)

Areas open when story missions are done. No area, mission or gun ever checks the player's level.

**In the story.** The Keeper's Lantern leans toward the nearest saint's light still burning in the world.
- When Abel lights it, it leans toward Ned's ember on the *Gilded Lily*.
- When an ember is paid to Sexton at the new moon, it goes into his ledger, out of the lantern's reach, and the flame swings to the next one.
- Sexton's Ledger records each bearing, and the station board lists a story place only once the lantern has found it. The next place exists for Ellis exactly when the story points there.
- Saint Ada's own ember should pull hardest of all, but the Deacon is fighting it and holding its light down. At the end of Act II he starts losing that fight, and for the first time the flame finds her at Gravesend.
- A fair-play seed: the flame never once leans toward a paid ember, and Ellis notices.

**In the game (new):**
- Each area is a `UAreaDefinition` data asset: its map, display name, enemy level band, the mission that opens it, its station and its rank promotion chances.
- The session save records completed missions, the active mission and step, opened areas, first boss defeats, and each map's world state (Skyreach's included).
- Every area has a station: a depot with a platform. Its station board, a LooterUI panel, lists every opened area, plus one blank line naming the mission that opens the next (for example “Finish ‘The Lantern Leans’”). Later areas aren't shown.
- After the first cast-off, every station board also lists “Skyreach (practice)” at any time: a plain transition (a fade, no cutscene) to Skyreach's jetty, whose board lists every opened area and returns the player to that area's station the same way. Skyreach is not an area of the story and needs no mission.
- The station board on Ransom's Rest, like Skyreach's jetty board, always lists every opened area, so travel can't strand the player.
- Side areas can open from side missions later, for example the wreck of the *Gilded Lily* for “The House Always Wins”.

**What levels still mean: difficulty, XP and loot level.**
- **Enemy levels.** Every area has an enemy level band. An enemy's level is the player's level plus −1, 0 or +1, kept inside the band, then plus its rank's offset. Bosses skip the ±1 roll: their level is the player's level kept inside the band, plus the offset. A player who rushes the story meets the band's floor; one who comes back strong meets its ceiling.
- **Health and damage.** Enemy health and damage grow linearly, ×(1 + 0.08 × (level − 1)). That is the rule guns already use (`UWeaponDefinition::DamagePerLevel` 0.08, existing), so a gun of the enemy's level always needs the same number of hits.
- **Loot level.** Dropped guns take the enemy's level. This is new: today every creature's drop is a level 1 gun, because the creature's level isn't passed to its loot.
- **Kill XP** = 10 × 1.08^(level − 1) × the rank's XP multiplier.
  - A level 1 basic kill gives 10 XP, as you set.
  - Kills per level grow slowly: 10 at level 1, about 20 at level 20 and about 60 at level 50. That lands the campaign near level 50.
  - Kill XP falls 15% for each level the enemy is below the player, down to 10%, so farming old areas isn't the fast road.
- **Mission XP** is a share of the current level's requirement: main missions 30%, side missions 20%.
- **Player health.** The enemies' damage growth assumes the player's health grows too. The proposed first level-up reward is +8% max health per level, to decide at the level step.
- **After the campaign** every area's band rises to follow the player to 70.

## Enemy ranks and legendary drops (new)

Legendary weapons can drop from anything that can die. They are rarer from basic creatures and increasingly common from Rare, Epic and Legendary monsters and bosses. It is built on what exists:
- `ULootTable`'s `WeaponDropChance`, `Min/MaxWeaponDrops` and `Luck` (existing).
- Each gun's rarity weights, 60/25/10/4/1 (existing).
- The luck rule in `UWeaponRollLibrary::RollRarityWith` (existing), which multiplies rarity tier *n*'s weight by (1 + Luck)^*n*.

Your rule that Common is the most likely rarity and each tier up is rarer holds at every rank. Luck above 1.4 would make Uncommon guns more likely than Common ones (and Epic passes Common near 1.5), so the tables stop at Luck 1.3 and give the top ranks more guns instead.

| Rare | “Restless”, blue | 8% of spawns on Ransom's Rest (up to 12% later), plus one placed in most scripted fights | Health ×2.5, damage ×1.2, XP ×2, size ×1.1, +1 level | `DA_LootTable_Rare` (new): 60%, 1 gun, Luck 0.5 | 2.2% (1 in 46) |
| Epic | “Gravebound”, purple | 2% on Ransom's Rest (up to 4% later), plus placed ones | Health ×5, damage ×1.4, XP ×4, size ×1.2, +1 level, one trait | `DA_LootTable_Epic` (new): every kill, 1–2 guns, Luck 1.0 | 11.8% (1 in 8.5) |
| Legendary | A name, or “Soulfed”; orange | Hand-placed on Ransom's Rest; 0.5% of spawns later; at most one alive per area | Health ×12, damage ×1.6, XP ×10, size ×1.4–1.8, +2 levels, a lair and one unique move | `DA_LootTable_Legendary` (new): every kill, 2 guns, Luck 1.3 | 21.4% (about 1 in 5) |

**Rules.**
- **Anything means anything.** Every enemy that can die, including boss adds, spiderlings and egg-sac spiders, uses at least the Basic table, so any kill can drop a legendary. The one exception is Skyreach on return visits, where by default creatures drop only ammo (see *Back to Skyreach for practice*).
- **Ranks show in soul-light.** Name tags use the rarity palette, so a purple tag means purple odds, and an Unpaid's coal burns in its rank's color (a basic one's is dull red, which is no rarity color). Rarity colors stay the game's one color code. Bosses also get a slim slanted bar at the top of the HUD.
- **Promotions** are rolled when the player arrives in an area. An arrival is coming in at its station, by train or back from Skyreach, or loading a session. A promoted creature that dies comes back as Basic.
- **No reload farming.** Each map saves when its promotions were last rolled and when its Legendary monster last died, counted in time played. An arrival rerolls promotions and brings the Legendary monster back only after 20 minutes of play, so Save & Quit then Continue doesn't reroll them.
- **Ammo** keeps your rule: every kill, leaning 2× to the kill gun's class. Ranked enemies drop more boxes through the existing `Min/MaxAmmoDrops`: Basic 1–2 (as today), Rare 2–3, Epic 3–4, Legendary 4–6, Boss 8–10.
- **Signature legendaries** (from the *Gilded Lily* on). On top of the boss table, each Hollow boss carries its soul-forged legendary (Dead Man's Hand, Whistler and so on). It is a separate roll after the boss table, guaranteed on the first defeat (or always 15%; your call when the *Lily* starts) and 15% on refights. It needs new `ULootTable` fields (the signature weapon, its chance, a first-defeat guarantee) and first defeats saved in the session. Ransom's Rest has no signature gun; its first named gun is Heirloom.
- **Refights.** Anything with a page in Sexton's Ledger can be called back to account, before the ending and after it. So Hollow bosses and Legendary monsters come back on a later arrival (after the 20 minutes), and the Ledger bounties of the endgame work the same way. Abel is fought once; he becomes a friend.
- **Tests and tools.**
  - `Looter.Loot.RankOdds` (new) simulates 200,000 kills per rank with a fixed seed. It checks that the legendary rate rises with rank, sits within 10% of the table, and that every rank table's rarity shares fall from Common to Legendary.
  - `Looter.Loot.SimulateDrops <rank> <kills>` (new) prints the counts so you can check the numbers yourself.
  - The existing DropOdds, DefaultTable and WeaponRarityOrder tests stay.

**Chests** (only when you OK bringing the backlog models in): a Supply Crate gives 1 gun at Luck 0.5 (3.7% legendary), a Strongbox 2 guns at Luck 1.0 (15.5%), and a saint's Reliquary opens after each Hollow falls.

## Plot

### Act I: Dust to Dust

1. **Ransom's Rest.** The skiff, the cold open and the grave. About fifteen minutes in, Sexton's deal on Ransom's Point, the lookout where Ellis died: bring each ember to this lookout at the new moon. Each one paid returns a little of Ellis's life (warmth, color, a heartbeat for a minute). Bring all seven and Ellis rests, *“and your father crosses.”* Then come the town, the dark chapel, the lantern in the Sink, and Pa at the burial boards. Abel lights the Keeper's Lantern from the ghost light he carries, and its flame leans north-east, over the ridges, toward Ned.
2. **The *Gilded Lily*.** Ned Purcell, the man who shot Abel, runs a floating casino where the house always wins. The house loses. His ember goes to Sexton at the new moon on Ransom's Point, and Ellis feels the sun on their skin for the first time since dying. The flame swings toward Dustwater, and never once toward Sexton. Ellis: *“Where'd Ned's go?”* Hob: *“Paid's paid, sunshine.”*
3. **Dustwater Mesa.** Ira Gale, inside his storm. Dying, he coughs: *“He paid us in grave gold, Ellis. The old kind they used to lay on dead men's eyes.”*
4. Ranger Ruth Calder ambushes Ellis outside Dustwater: dead or not, Ellis rode with the Dunne Gang. Then the Unpaid pour out of the dust and the two of them fight back to back. It's an uneasy truce. At the new moon Ruth watches from the bluff path while Ellis pays Ira's ember, and the flame swings toward the Mire.

### Act II: The Unpaid

5. **Saint Agnes Mire.** Sister Constance raises the dead because she thinks she's helping them, since they can't cross. Before the end she tells Ellis: *“The saints don't hold souls back, child. They light the way. Put them out and the dead can't find the shore. And somebody's waiting at the gate to sell them directions.”*
6. **Furnace Hollow.** Barrels Kessler, burning from the inside out. His foundry is full of the Unpaid of the town he robbed.
7. **The Needles.** Lena Okoro describes the man who hired them: tall, polite, a stovepipe hat. Abel's line at the boards (*“a tall fella on the rail who never lifted a finger”*) and Sexton's own (*“I attend every death”*) click into place.
8. Hob confesses. He was a Revenant once, eighty years ago. He paid his debt in full, and Sexton made him a crow. *“Every Revenant he makes ends up with feathers, sunshine. Ask me how I know.”* Lena's ember is paid anyway; Ellis isn't ready to break the deal yet. The flame swings toward the Quarry.
9. **Stoneweight Quarry.** Tobias Grant doesn't want to fight. He offers his ember if Ellis lets his crew walk. Ellis can accept, but Sexton's Mourners arrive and force the fight anyway.
10. **The new moon.** Ellis comes to Ransom's Point with the sixth ember and refuses to hand it over. Sexton stops being polite. He takes it, and Ellis dies again. They wake in the family plot beside Pa's frosted grave, greyer and colder than before, with Ransom's Rest gone dark. Sexton now holds six saints. He needs one more: Saint Ada's, in the Deacon's pocket. The Deacon is losing his fight with it, and for the first time the lantern leans toward Gravesend.

### Act III: The Gravewind

11. Ellis, Ruth, Tilly and Hob make a plan: reach the Deacon before Sexton does, keep Saint Ada's ember, and take the other six back at the Toll Gate.
12. **Gravesend.** The necropolis, crawling with the Unpaid. The Deacon is dying in a mausoleum, holding Saint Ada's ember to his chest. Ellis and the Deacon finally talk. There are no excuses and no secret reasons, just a coward's confession and a kid who came home, which echoes Ellis's headboard: *“Came home at the last.”* The Mourners attack, and for one fight the Deacon stands beside Ellis again.
13. **The Hollow Deacon.** He dies, and Sexton climbs into his body. Ellis has to fight their mentor's corpse while his own voice keeps apologizing. When it's over, Saint Ada's ember is in Ellis's hand.
14. **The Toll Gate.** At dusk Ellis walks out past Pa's board on Gravewind Point. Abel says *“Go on, El. I held the door this long.”* Ellis steps off into the canyon and rides the Gravewind west, down the Sundown Road to where it leaves the world. There is a pier of grey wood on a sea of fog, a lantern, and every unpaid soul in the Reaches standing in line. Sexton at the gate is a towering ferryman made of black cloth and crows, with a long rifle called Toll. Hob flies at his face.

### Ending

The six embers spill free of Sexton's ledger, and Ellis carries them and Saint Ada's home: seven saints relit, and seven towns whose dead can finally cross. The Gravewind blows, and thousands of souls walk past Ellis onto the light laid across the fog, toward the Far Shore. The Deacon is among them, and he tips his hat. Abel walks past last and just nods. Ellis's debt is paid. They could go too.

They don't. Sexton's ledger, now in Ellis's hand, is full of souls who slipped away while the saints were dark. *“Not yet.”* Hob settles on their shoulder. Grandma Delia sets a plate.

## Side quests (samples)

- **Unfinished Business** (Ransom's Rest). Amos Whitlock, an Unpaid farmer, won't rest until his hay is in. Load it, under fire.
- **Burial Board.** A town lost its undertaker. Escort its dead to the Gravewind while the Bone Hounds circle.
- **Ruth's Brother.** Ruth's brother rode with a rival gang and died Hollow. She wants him found and put down by the only hand that can.
- **The House Always Wins.** A card game with the dead on the wreck of the *Gilded Lily*. The pot is a legendary; the stakes are your next life. It opens the wreck as a side area.

## Loot in this world

- **Who makes the guns.** Frontier makers: Coachman and Breacher shotguns, Regulator rifles, Homestead farm guns, Kestrel carbines. Out here guns come off the dead and get rebuilt by whoever's handy, so every gun is a patchwork of other guns' parts. The Hollow's guns are **soul-forged irons**: weapons that killed so much that the dead stayed in them.
- **Rarity and the beams.** Everything a dead hand held keeps a little of its owner, and Ellis sees it burn. The loot beam is that leftover soul-light, and its color is how much of the owner's will stayed in the gun.
- **Ranks.** The same soul-light shows on the living and the dead. A creature that has fed longer on the dark glows blue, purple or orange, and it is likelier to carry soul-forged iron. That's why a legendary can drop from anything but comes far more often from Rares, Epics, Legendaries and bosses.
- **Chests.** Supply Crates are Ruth's Ranger caches. Strongboxes are safes from the gang's old bank jobs. Reliquaries are the saints themselves: each opens for Ellis the moment its ember is pried out of a Hollow, as the saint's thanks, before the ember has to be handed on. The Reliquary of Ransom's Rest lies smashed and dark in the chapel. It's the last one Ellis gets back.
- **Heirloom.** Abel's Ranchhand pump shotgun, a named Epic with fixed parts and the line *Hold the door.* A keeper is buried with his lantern, not his iron, and Abel's lantern wasn't on him, so Delia kept the gun back. She gives it to Ellis after the boss. It's the game's first named gun, and his ghost carries its spectral twin.
- **Elements (when they come).** **Ember** (fire), **Grave** (rot over time, with bonus damage against the Hollow), **Wind** (knockback toward edges) and **Spirit** (bonus damage against the Unpaid; passes through thin cover).

## Endgame (levels 50–70)

- **The Ledger.** Repeatable bounties on souls who escaped while the saints were dark: named Hollow outlaws with rolled traits, each with a chance at a soul-forged legendary. Bounty tiers set the danger. Every area's band rises to follow the player.
- **The Gravesend Deeps.** Catacombs under the necropolis, built from a room kit, deeper and deadlier floor by floor.
- **The Midnight Stage.** Escort a ghostly stagecoach of the dead along the Reaches' roads to the Gravewind while the Unpaid try to drag its passengers off.
- **Hanging Moon.** A recurring night on Ransom's Rest when the dead walk the orchard. Hold the graves until dawn. It uses the endgame band.

## Production notes

- **Reuses:** the western art kit as it is (farm, cabins, windmill, fences, rocks, cliffs, trees), the spider and the slime, both gun bases, and `UMissionSubsystem` with the minimap arrow. Graves, crosses and burial boards are cheap props, and the Reliquary is the story's centerpiece.
- **New:**
  - The Unpaid, a floating figure on its own small rig: no legs, so no walk cycle and no navmesh. It is masked and dithered with an emissive coal, never translucent, and priced on Medium before anything else.
  - Missions as data, sessions that travel (with the practice trip to Skyreach and back), enemy ranks and level bands, a boss framework, captions and a dusk lighting state.
  - The railroad: a locomotive, a passenger car and the hearse car, a rail track kit, the depot with its platform, a water tower and a signal; the station board (a LooterUI panel); and the train's arrival and departure, a short arrival shot or a fade. The skiff model is still needed for the tutorial exit and the gang's skiff, in two paints (packet and dark).
  - Eight bespoke boss fights (the seven and Sexton) plus Abel: the main cost.
  - A grounded map. Ransom's Rest is the first map that isn't an island, so the terrain scripts get a grounded setting beside the island one, and the tutorial island must still come out identical. The terrain runs past the valley up the ridges and down the Rim into Gravewind Canyon. The horizon is cheap: canyon walls, plains and far mesas, unlit, with no collision and no masked leaves. Everything past the valley gets at most 30 draws and 0.6 ms of GPU at the heaviest view, Ransom's Point. The sky follows: the cloud dome grows past the horizon, the fog turns warm, and the sun sits low in the west.
  - An invisible playable outline (`APlayableArea`) backs up the cliffs and steep slopes. Going off one of its open edges starts Hob's catch, and the minimap covers only the valley.
  - Cliffs and obstacles to break up the open ground. Most come from the existing kit, instanced: cliff faces stood up as outcrops, boulders, trees, fences and ruins. New: a freestanding outcrop kit, and stone walls and hedgerows. A check in the terrain scripts measures open ground: under 10% of the walkable floor more than 20 m from anything, outside ground left open on purpose (the hayfields, Main Street).
- **Cheap paths first:**
  - The townsfolk of Ransom's Rest talk through their doors and windows, so only Sexton and Hob need models there.
  - The cold open is a code-driven camera with silhouettes.
  - Captions only, with no voice acting.
  - The Grave Sight vision mode (a post-process) stays a later, measured option.
- **Risk:** medium. The boss roster is big, but each boss anchors one area and one set of enemies, so the work comes in clean pieces. The first humans (Sexton, then the outlaws' followings on the *Lily*) set the art bar; concepts come first. The grounded map is new terrain work: the seam where the valley meets the far terrain, long views from Ransom's Point, and long shadows from a low sun. It is proved on a small test layout before Ransom's Rest uses it.

## Why pick it, and what to watch

It's built like a looter shooter. There are seven names on a list from the first quarter hour, and each one is a place, a boss and a legendary. The player always knows what's next and why, and the lantern makes “why can I go there now” part of the story. It fits the art already built, and its supernatural layer adds a whole enemy family without a second art kit. Abel gives the first hour its heart.

Watch out:
- It's darker than the sunny farm suggests. That contrast is the hook, so own it.
- A father as the first boss is heavy for hour one. Hob's humor and the grief mechanic carry it.
- “The patron is the villain” has to be seeded fairly: the man on the railing, Abel's line, Ira's grave gold, the flame that never leans toward a paid ember, Constance and Lena.

## Decisions

The six questions of step 1, decided on 2026-10-01:

1. **The name of Ellis's home: Ransom's Rest.** The place and its town are both Ransom's Rest, and locals call it “the Rest”. The town's main street is just “Main Street”. In the game: the level `/Game/Maps/Lvl_RansomsRest`, the layout folder `Art/Levels/RansomsRest` and the area asset `DA_Area_RansomsRest`. The documents are `Docs/Story.md` (this one) and `Docs/Areas/RansomsRest.md` (the first area).
2. **When Sexton makes the deal: early**, about 15 minutes in, on the lookout where Ellis died. The list of seven names lands in the first quarter hour.
3. **The first boss: Abel Ransom**, Ellis's father, as one of the Unpaid. The Gravemother stays as the Legendary monster's side mission.
4. **The tutorial exit: the cloud match cut.** Your skiff sails into a cloud, and it parts on the gang's skiff arriving at Ransom's Rest seven days ago, with you as Ellis. Skyreach is never named in the story.
5. **Skyreach stays reachable as a practice island.** The first cast-off plays the cloud match cut and the cold open. After that, every station board (see 8) lists “Skyreach (practice)” at any time, a plain transition with no cutscene to Skyreach's jetty, whose board lists every opened area to go back. Skyreach stays outside the story, its creatures give 0 XP, and by default they drop only ammo on return visits (a default you can change). New sessions still offer “Skip the tutorial”. See *Back to Skyreach for practice*.
6. **The tutorial's guns come along** into the story as the guns Ellis was buried with. With “Skip the tutorial”, Ellis starts with a Common Bullpup in the coffin.

Step 2, the map, decided on 2026-10-01:

7. **The layout of Ransom's Rest is approved, with two changes.** Ransom's Rest is not an island: the story takes place on **Teropa**, an earth-like planet, and Ransom's Rest is grounded on it. And the open ground gets cliffs and obstacles to break it up. Skyreach stays a floating island, outside the story.

The six questions your step 2 changes raised, decided on 2026-10-01: four approved as proposed, travel changed to the train, and the *Gilded Lily* deferred.

8. **Travel between regions: by train** (your change; the proposal was small gas-bag airships). In your words: *“I think fast travel between regions should be via train.”* Regions are the game's areas. The railroad carries people, freight and Ellis between them. Every area has a station, a depot with a platform, and its station board (a LooterUI panel) lists every opened area, plus one blank line naming the mission that opens the next, and “Skyreach (practice)” after the first cast-off. Coming in at a station, by train or back from Skyreach, or loading a session, is an arrival. On Ransom's Rest the line comes in through Stage Gap, a rail cut through a saddle in the East Ridge, from the wooded valley beyond, and the depot stands at the east end of town by the undertaker's yard. Ellis rides in the hearse car, Tilly's late father's funeral car. Skiffs are rare: the tutorial's on Skyreach, and the Dunne Gang's, an outlaw's airship bought with Sexton's grave gold, which brings the gang to the hidden mooring under Ransom's Point in the cold open. The cloud match cut is unchanged, and the skiff model has two paints, packet for the tutorial and dark for the gang. A practice trip to Skyreach and back is a plain transition, a default you can change. See *Getting around* and *Back to Skyreach for practice*.
9. **The ground: a high valley that ends at the Rim.** Ridges close it on the north, east and south. On the west the Rim drops 70 m into Gravewind Canyon, with empty plains running to the sunset beyond. Everything that hung over the island's rim keeps its drop with the layout unchanged: Ransom's Point and the gang's hidden mooring, the deck on Gravewind Point, and Mill Falls, now into a gorge through the Hogback. The ridges' cliff bands and the canyon give the cliffs you asked for.
10. **Where the dead go: west, down the Sundown Road.** The road leaves the world where the sun goes down and ends at the Toll Gate, a grey pier on a sea of fog, with the Far Shore across it. The saints' light lays a way across the fog; without it the dead need the ferryman. The Toll Gate is on no map, and Ellis still reaches it by stepping off Gravewind Point at dusk.
11. **The body after its night on the board: back to its grave at dawn.** The keeper sits the night with it by lantern light, the wind takes the soul at dusk, and at dawn the keeper carries the body home. It makes “the Unpaid drift home to their graves” literal, fits Abel's body in his frosted grave, and keeps the Bone Hounds, which raid the boards on nights no keeper sits up.
12. **The *Gilded Lily*: deferred until Ransom's Rest is complete.** In your words: *“Wait until Ransom's Rest is complete.”* Whether it stays an airship is open. Until then this document calls it a paddle-wheel casino, notes the airship as written (tethered high over a deep canyon, above the cloud that fills it each morning) and keeps its fight as written: the falls, the rigging, the gondolas and the Sky Jumpers. The other option is still a casino riverboat on the big river, which loses the falls, the gondolas and the Sky Jumpers. Either way, the train brings Ellis to its area's station.
13. **Names: keep the Reaches and the Rim Rangers.** The Reaches is the western frontier of Teropa's great continent, where settlement ends, and the Rim is its rimrock edge, which the Rangers ride and Ransom's Rest stands on. Past the Rim the plains run empty to the sunset, because nobody settles the land the dead cross. The Far Shore stays, and the Sundown Road is new. Teropa is named in the docs and, on screen, as the session picker's subtitle under each session (“Teropa · Ransom's Rest”, step 8), and rarely in dialogue.

Next is building Ransom's Rest. You asked for it to be built by several agents at once, starting with the terrain tools for a grounded valley (step 3a on). The *Gilded Lily* question comes back when Ransom's Rest is complete. Later decisions are asked at their own build step (see *Ransom's Rest: the first area*, `Docs/Areas/RansomsRest.md`).
