# 4. Leviathan

> *Your island isn't an island. It's breathing. And the hunters have found it.*

The islands of the sky aren't islands. They're titans, beasts so old and slow that forests and towns have grown on
their backs, and one by one they're waking. Sloane Teague, a harpooner who deserted the order sworn to kill every titan
before it wakes, has been hiding on Skyreach as a farmhand. Then the ground under the farm takes a breath.

## Cold open

Three years ago, on the bow of a harpoon frigate. A titan called **Marrowdeep** fills the sky, an eye as big as a lake
slowly turning toward the ship. A young harpooner raises the Lance. A woman's voice, calm: *"Now, Sloane."* Then,
from somewhere in the moss on the titan's back, a small high cry: a calf. The harpooner lowers the Lance. Everything
goes wrong at once: ropes snap, men fall, the frigate lists, and the titan screams as other harpoons hit home.

Today: Sloane wakes in a farm bunkhouse on Skyreach, soaked in sweat. Through the window: wheat, a windmill, a perfect
afternoon. A cup of water on the sill trembles, all by itself.

## The world

- **The titans.** Beings the size of islands drift on the high winds, sleeping for centuries at a time. Soil, rivers,
  forests and towns gather on their backs. Most people have no idea their island is alive. Old folks know it "breathes"
  once a year, rising and settling a meter on the night of the **Long Breath** festival.
- **Amber.** A titan's heart is a mass of glowing orange crystal that makes it lighter than air. Amber also lights
  lamps, lifts airships and goes into the finest guns.
- **Stillbacks.** Dead titans turn to stone, their amber dims, and they slowly sink. The great cities are built on
  stillbacks, and the cities are sinking with them.
- **The Listeners** were the old priests who could hear titan-song and guide the beasts. Their order is all but gone;
  their monasteries are ruins.
- **The Spear** began as whalers, harvesting amber from dead titans. Forty-five years ago, a titan named **Bellwether**
  woke beneath the city of **Harrowgate** and rolled over. Ten thousand people fell. Since Ysolde took command, the Spear
  has been a crusade: kill every titan before it wakes, even if people live on its back. *A quick fall is a mercy next to a
  waking.*
- **The secret.** The titans are waking because **the Migration** has come. Once every few centuries they travel to the
  **Calving Grounds** at the edge of the sky to give birth and to die. A titan that wakes gently keeps its back level
  and carries its people with it. A frightened titan rolls. And nothing frightens titans like the death-scream of
  another titan's amber. The Spear is causing the disaster it fights.

## Protagonist: Sloane Teague

Sloane was the best harpooner of their generation, Ysolde Harrowgate's pick, with eleven titan kills before they were
twenty-five. During the hunt of Marrowdeep they heard what no harpooner should, a calf crying, and froze. The hunt went
wrong, crewmates died, and Marrowdeep died anyway. Sloane deserted that night. For three years they've worked the
Abernathy farm on Skyreach under a false name, trying to forget the song they keep hearing in their sleep.

Sloane is a latent **Listener**. The arc runs from hiding to guarding, from killer to Listener, from guilt to the
harder thing, which is doing better. They start the game already deadly: the tutorial is an old soldier finding out
their hands still remember.

> *"I've killed eleven titans. I can still hear every one of them."*

## Antagonist: Captain-General Ysolde Harrowgate

Ysolde was nine when Bellwether woke under Harrowgate. Her father threw her aboard the last airship leaving as the
city tipped into the sky. She took the city's name, joined the whalers, and turned them into the Spear. Now in her
fifties, grey and iron-straight, with one hand replaced by a harpoon-launcher, she lights ten thousand candles every
year on the anniversary. Her crews would die for her.

She doesn't hate titans. She sees them as floods with a heartbeat, natural disasters waiting to happen to everyone
living on their backs, and she has watched a waking. Her methods are monstrous: she kills titans with towns still on
them, after an evacuation if there's time. Her logic is hard to argue with, until Sloane learns the Spear is making
the wakings worse. Her final goal is to kill **the Sovereign**, the eldest titan, the size of a country, with the
**Leviathan Lance**, a cannon that drives a spike of hardened amber into a titan's heart. Kill the Sovereign and the
Migration ends forever.

Sloane was her protégé. She still calls Sloane her best lance, and she'd still take them back.

> *"I don't hate them, Sloane. I just love us more."*

### Her lieutenants (bosses)

- **Flensemaster Corwin Mott** runs the Driftmoor flensing yards, where titans are cut up for amber. A butcher who
  loves his saws.
- **Bosun Greta Brannock** commands the harpoon frigate *Penitent*. Practical, loyal, not cruel.
- **Lamplighter Ines Vex** is the Spear's amber alchemist. She has learned to make dead titans move again with amber
  injections, as weapons: **the Stitched**.
- **Lancemaster Odran Hask** guards the Leviathan Lance's foundry at Harrowgate Spire.

## Enemies and creatures

- **The Spear**: Harpooners whose shots pin you to walls, Flensers with saw-blade cleavers and shotguns, Lancers with
  heavy amber-charged rifles, **Ropers** who grapple you off ledges, Chandlers who burn amber oil, Spotters on gliders,
  and frigates as set pieces.
- **The Rendermen**, amber poachers who use traps, mines and explosives and run when it goes badly.
- **The Stitched**: dead titans and their parasites, made to move again by amber injections. Wrong-moving and
  stitched with cable.
- **Titan ecosystems.** Every titan is a world, with fauna that lives on it:
  - Meadow Wolf spiders are titan-mites that keep the fur-meadows clean and swarm when their titan is hurt.
  - MossBacks live on mossy titans. Cliff Huntsmen lie flat on rocky hides. Ember Tarantulas gather where amber breaks
    the surface. Sky Jumpers leap between titans.
  - **Lampreys** the size of horses latch onto titans; **Barnacle Crabs** armor their hides; **Sky Rays** glide
    between herds.
- **Bosses beyond the lieutenants:** the **Mawlamprey**, a parasite as long as a train burrowed into Thornback; the
  **Rimewyrm** in Coldback's ice caves; the **Stitched titan** in the Gullet.

## Skyreach in this story

1. **Move.** Sloane wakes in the Abernathy bunkhouse. Chores.
2. **Follow the road to the village.** The Crossing is getting ready for the Long Breath festival tomorrow.
3. **The gun rack.** Sloane's real weapon is buried under the bunkhouse floor. The rack holds the farm's varmint rifle.
   Ma Abernathy: *"Spiders have gone mad in the grove. Take the rifle."*
4. **The dummies.** Tin cans and scarecrows. Ma watches Sloane shoot: *"Where does a farmhand learn to shoot like
   that?"*
5. **The spiders.** In the grove the spiders are swarming around something hurt: a titan calf with a broken harpoon in
   its side.
6. **The loadout.** Sloane checks the rifle, and thinks about the bunkhouse floor.
7. **The lookout.** A Spear frigate on the horizon, tracking the calf. Then the whole island heaves: Old Hollow's
   breath comes a day early. The ground tilts a few degrees, the waterfall swings, and in the village every bell rings
   on its own.

## Plot

### Act I: The Long Breath (levels 1–15)

1. The tutorial, ending with Old Hollow's breath. Sloane names the calf **Biscuit**, mostly to annoy Dr. Quist later.
2. The Spear lands on Skyreach the next morning to "evacuate" the village before they kill the island. Their captain
   recognizes Sloane. Sloane digs up their old lance-rifle, and the farm fights. The Spear pulls back to wait for
   orders.
3. Maren Ashdown tells Sloane what Old Hollow is, and what the breath means: *"She isn't waking angry, child. She's
   waking to leave. The Migration has come."* To guide a waking, Sloane needs the Listeners' **Songbook**, last kept in
   a monastery on Coldback. The way there starts at Driftmoor.
4. **Driftmoor.** Sneak through the whaling port inside a dead titan's skull. Rescue Dr. Quist from Mott's flensing
   yard, where she was caught studying a "specimen." She takes one look at Biscuit and joins for good.
5. **The Grazer Herd.** The *Penitent* is hunting a grazing herd. Sloane runs across the moving backs, cuts harpoon
   lines and fights Brannock on her own deck. As the herd escapes, it sings, and for the first time Sloane hears it
   clearly, not as a nightmare but as music.
6. A letter arrives by glider, in Ysolde's square hand: *"Come home, Sloane. I'll forgive the desertion. I need my best
   lance for the Sovereign."* Sloane burns it.

### Act II: The Song (levels 15–36)

7. **Thornback.** An uneasy deal with Kip Ortolan's poachers to cross the desert titan. A Mawlamprey has burrowed in
   near its heart; killing it lets the titan sing again. Kip sells Sloane out once, apologizes twice, and turns up
   later anyway.
8. **Coldback.** The Listener monastery in the ice, the Rimewyrm in the caves below, and the Songbook. Sloane learns to
   use an amber **resonator**, a singing stone that calms or steers creatures. Maren's lessons begin to work. The
   Songbook also says what the Listeners feared: a titan's death-scream terrifies every titan that hears it. The Spear
   makes the violent wakings.
9. **The Gullet.** Inside a dead titan, Ines Vex's laboratory and her proudest work: a Stitched titan, fought from the
   inside as it moves. Her notes reveal the plan. The Leviathan Lance is finished, and its target is the Sovereign,
   the eldest, whose song leads the whole Migration. If it dies screaming, a thousand titans wake at once in terror.
10. **Old Hollow wakes.** The Spear strikes Skyreach as it wakes for good. Sloane stands on the lookout, the titan's
    brow, singing it calm through the resonator while Spear boarders come over the cliffs. Old Hollow rises, slowly,
    and keeps its back level. Nobody falls. The farm, the village and the windmill all ride a living titan into the
    sky and join the Migration. From here on, the hub travels.

### Act III: The Migration (levels 36–50)

11. **Harrowgate Spire.** Storm the Spear's fortress to stop the Lance, and arrive a day late: Ysolde's flagship, the
    *Vigil*, has already sailed with it. Free the captive Listeners in the cells, beat Lancemaster Hask, and take the
    Lance's firing schematics.
12. **The Sovereign.** It rises out of a cloud bank like a continent, with whole ruined cities on its back and the
    Migration's herd gathered around it. Ysolde's fleet attacks. The final battle crosses the Sovereign's back as it
    wakes, Old Hollow's villagers firing from their own titan alongside it.
13. **Ysolde.** On the Lance platform, in her harpoon rig, while the Lance charges. She fights like Sloane's teacher,
    because she was. Stop the charge, survive her, and the Sovereign sings.

### Ending

Ysolde hangs from the wrecked Lance platform as the Sovereign rises. Sloane can pull her up. If saved, she watches the
Migration pass in silence, then lights a single candle. Or Sloane can let the Spear's commander fall the way Harrowgate
fell.

The Migration reaches the Calving Grounds. In the last scene, the farmers of Skyreach watch from the lookout as Old
Hollow gives birth to a calf the size of a hill. Biscuit floats out to meet its kind, turns back once, and stays with
Sloane.

## Side quests (samples)

- **The Long Breath.** Batten the Crossing down before the island breathes: barns, bells, loose roofs, a stubborn goat.
- **Lost Calf.** A grazer calf has drifted from its herd. Find the herd and fight poachers off it.
- **Barnacle Season.** A friendly titan is covered in Barnacle Crabs and Lampreys. Clean it, in combat, deep in its
  fur.
- **The Last Harpoon.** Sloane's old crewmate, now a Spear captain, wants to defect, but his family lives in Harrowgate.

## Loot in this world

- **Who makes the guns.** The whaling trade built the sky's heaviest guns. Regulator rifles are Spear issue,
  Thunderdrum makes deck harpoons and heavy shotguns, Homestead is the farmer's gun, Scrapjack is the poacher's, and
  Kestrel makes light guns for glider spotters. Crews mend their guns at sea with whatever parts are aboard, so a
  harpooner's rifle is usually three other rifles.
- **Rarity and the beams.** Rarity is amber purity. A gun set with amber flares when it's freed from its owner, and
  that's the loot beam. Orange is heart-amber, a chip of a titan's heart.
- **Chests.** Supply Crates are Spear provisions. Strongboxes are amber-trade safes. Reliquaries are Listener shrines,
  each with a **titan's tear** (a chip of a dead titan's heart) floating over the lid.
- **Elements (when they come).** **Amber** (lift: targets float), Frost, Venom (parasite-borne) and Shock (storm-born
  titans).
- **A new weapon type to consider:** the harpoon launcher, which pins enemies to walls, yanks them off ledges, or pulls
  Sloane across a gap between titans.

## Endgame (levels 50–70)

- **The Calving Grounds.** Poachers and the Stitched come for newborn titans. Defend the calvings in waves.
- **Stitched Titans.** Raid-scale fights on corrupted dead titans: climb them while they move and destroy the amber
  injectors. The game's biggest set pieces.
- **Gullet Dives.** Descents inside dead titans built from a kit of body chambers, deeper and harder each floor.
- **Poacher Contracts.** Hunt named Rendermen captains for their hoards.
- **Herd Escort.** Guide a migrating herd through a storm with the resonator, under attack.

## Production notes

- **Moving islands are an illusion.** The titan you stand on is a static level. Motion comes from the sky dome and the
  distant titans moving, scrolling clouds, wind, and a slow camera sway. A "breath" is a camera shake and a sky shift.
  Never move the level's collision.
- **Creature-first:** the rig pipeline that made the spider (Blender rig, code-driven motion) carries the whole cast.
  Distant titans are huge low-detail silhouettes, cheap to draw.
- **Colossus fights** (climbing a moving creature) are the most expensive feature here. Make only a few, and set the
  first one inside the Gullet, where the "moving" titan is an interior.
- **New:** harpoon and grapple traversal; the resonator (calm or redirect creatures); interior body levels.
- **Risk:** high, and the most original premise of the eight.

## Why pick it, and what to watch

Pick it for the most original premise here, a creature-first world that gives the bestiary a reason to exist, huge
marketable images, and a villain with a real argument. Watch out: giant moving creatures are expensive, so keep the
illusion cheap and the colossus fights few. A gentle titan-hugging story can lose the shooter, but the Spear, the
poachers and the Stitched keep the guns busy.
