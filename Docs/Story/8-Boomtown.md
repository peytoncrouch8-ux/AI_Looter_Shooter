# 8. Boomtown

> *The greatest gunsmith who ever lived hid her twelve masterpieces across the sky and left a will. Every greedy
> relative, gun-crazed cult and tycoon alive just bought a ticket.*

Henrietta "Hettie" Bellweather, a hundred and three, blew up her barn for the forty-second time and didn't walk out. Her
will says whoever brings her twelve hidden **Masterworks** home to Skyreach inherits Bellweather Arms. Her family, a
gun-worshipping cult, a tax man and the Gun King of Concord are racing for them. So is Sunny Pickett, the farmhand who
fed her chickens.

## Cold open

The barn on Skyreach, at night. A tiny old woman in welding goggles tightens one last bolt on something with too many
barrels. *"Mr. Pockets, take notes. Prototype forty-two. If this works, I'll be famous. If it doesn't, I'll be famous
and dead."* The training dummy in the corner says nothing, as always. KA-BOOM. The roof lifts off and settles back, mostly. A chicken
lands on the weathervane.

Next morning: a funeral in the orchard. Sunny Pickett in a borrowed suit. The coffin is closed. *"Obviously,"* murmurs
the lawyer.

## The world

- **The sky runs on guns.** Every island has a gunsmith, and the big ones are dynasties: **Bellweather Arms**
  (Hettie's: the finest), **Vandersloot Firearms & Fireworks** (cheap and everywhere), **Regulator & Daughters**
  (precision), **Kestrel Aeronautic** (bullpups for sky crews), **Scrapjack Salvage Co.** (welded from junk),
  **Thunderdrum Ordnance** (loud) and **Zephyr Atelier** (art you can shoot).
- **The Bellweather Standard.** Seventy years ago Hettie invented interchangeable gun parts: any barrel on any receiver,
  any stock on any gun. Every maker in the sky uses her standard, which is why every gun is built from parts. In a
  sense, every gun in the sky is a Bellweather.
- **Hettie** herself: inventor, war hero, prankster, five times married ("four husbands and one very confused rival"),
  and the only gunsmith who ever made a gun that fires preserves. She lived and worked on Skyreach. The barn is her
  workshop, the windmill drives the lathe in the shed beneath it, the target meadow is her test range, and the dummies are her prototypes.
- **The Masterworks.** Twelve legendary guns built across her life, each one hidden inside a trial she built (puzzles,
  traps, a guardian), each themed on a chapter of her life. Rumor says there's a thirteenth, **the Last Word**: a gun
  to end all guns.
- **The secret.** Hettie isn't dead. She faked it to draw out the one man who'd found out the Last Word is real.

## Protagonist: Sunny Pickett

Sunny has been Hettie's farmhand for six years: fed the chickens, mended the fences, caught falling prototypes,
endured the jokes. Cheerful, unflappable, wildly underestimated, can't cook. Sunny is also, without knowing it, one of
the best shots in the sky, because every chore Hettie ever gave them somehow involved a gun.

The will names Sunny as a contender alongside the family, and the family is not pleased. The arc runs from "just the
help" to Hettie's true heir, and from a people-pleaser who says yes to everyone to someone who will say no to a
tycoon's face, and to Hettie's.

> *"I'm not here for the company. I'm here because she still owes me six years of chicken feed."*

## Antagonist: Mortimer Vandersloot

The Gun King of Concord: flamboyant, enormous, mustached, his face stenciled on every crate he ships. Hettie's
business partner sixty years ago and her third husband for eleven months. He founded **Vandersloot Firearms &
Fireworks** on one idea: *a gun in every pot*. Make them cheap, make them fast, make them break so people buy more. He
despises craftsmanship as a luxury for people who can't sell. Vandersloot guns are the grey commons you find in every
crate in the game.

He wants the Masterworks to copy them by the million. More than that, he wants **the Last Word**, the gun Hettie built
fifty years ago, whose single shot jams every gun in range for good. Fired on every rival factory, it would leave
exactly one gunmaker in the sky. He's a comic villain with a real, very modern menace: a monopoly.

> *"Quality, my dear, is a luxury for people who can't sell."*

## Enemies and creatures

- **The Vanderslootin' Boys**: Vandersloot's security in matching red uniforms with his face on their helmets.
  Riflemen, Shield Salesmen, and the **Fireworks Division**: rocket-men, Roman-candle gunners and Catherine Wheel's
  spinning heavies.
- **The Calibre gang**: Big Mama's bandits, mostly her relatives.
- **The cousins' mercenaries**: whatever Percival and Prudence's money could hire this week.
- **The Holy Recoil**: zealots who fire blessed rounds and preach between reloads.
- **The Rejects**: Hettie's failed prototypes, animated, ornery and still trying to work.
- **Clockwork horses** from Doc Spark's lab.
- **Creatures**: Meadow Wolf spiders that keep getting into the blasting powder, MossBacks at Honeymoon Falls, Cliff
  Huntsmen on the Holy Recoil's mesa, Ember Tarantulas in the Folly's smoldering junk, Sky Jumpers at the County Fair.
  And chickens. Hettie's chickens are not normal chickens.

## Skyreach in this story

1. **Move.** The morning after the funeral. The farm is very quiet. The chickens still need feeding.
2. **Follow the road to the village.** The Crossing, where everyone has an opinion on who gets the farm.
3. **The gun rack.** The rack holds Hettie's "chore gun," the rifle she handed Sunny for every errand.
4. **The dummies.** Sunny hits the old dummy, back on the range where it belongs, and for the first time in six years
   it talks: *"Ow. Sloppy. She'd have hated that grouping."* Meet Mr. Pockets, properly.
5. **The spiders.** They've gotten into the blasting powder again.
6. **The loadout.** Hettie's goggles are in Sunny's coat pocket, a gift from the week before she died. Put them on, and
   every gun's stats appear.
7. **The lookout.** Airships are coming from every direction: every relative, gun nut and opportunist in the sky,
   arriving for the reading of the will.

## Plot

### Act I: The Will (levels 1–15)

1. The tutorial, ending with the airships arriving.
2. **The reading of the will.** The barn. Quill reads. The cousins sneer. Vandersloot shows up uninvited with a
   marching band. Then the will names Sunny, and the room explodes. Hettie's phonograph recording plays: *"If you're
   hearing this, I'm dead, or hiding from the Auditor. Either way..."* She lays out the hunt, and the first clue sends
   everyone running for the windmill. First Love is under it, and the brawl follows everyone out of the barn and up the hill.
3. **The County Fair.** The trial is a carnival: trick shooting galleries, a hall of mirrors, a Ferris wheel. Big Mama
   Calibre and her boys want the prize. Blue Ribbon.
4. **Honeymoon Falls.** The honeymoon suite holds two Masterworks and a lot of feelings. Percival and Prudence attack
   in a two-seat walking contraption that mostly works.
5. Vandersloot makes Sunny an offer over dinner: *"Name your price."* Sunny thinks about it. *"How much for the
   chickens?"* He doesn't get it.

### Act II: The Hunt (levels 15–38)

6. **The Front.** Colonel Gus, ex-husband number two, has guarded Hettie's war trial for forty years out of love.
   Hettie's recordings of the war years are the first time Sunny hears her sad. Then **Ol' Bessie**, the walking
   artillery piece she built for the war, wakes up.
7. **Patent Row.** The Auditor catches up. The divorce-court trial is a courtroom shooting gallery (cardboard lawyers,
   real bullets) for Alimony. Doc Spark's clockwork horses get loose. Then the Auditor fights Sunny himself, and he is
   terrifying. Beaten, he finds something better to do: Sunny shows him Vandersloot's hidden ledgers in the patent
   office. *"Mr. Vandersloot,"* he says to no one, *"we need to discuss your deductions."*
8. **The Holy Recoil.** The cult has Rainmaker on its altar as a holy relic. Brother Kickback fights in recoil-powered
   armor: *"The Lord giveth recoil, and the Lord taketh away."*
9. **Something's off.** Hettie's recordings keep mentioning things that happened after she died: the Auditor's ugly
   tie, the cousins' contraption. Mr. Pockets gets cagey.
10. **The raid.** Vandersloot hits Skyreach with everything. The workshop burns, the Masterworks are stolen, and Mr.
    Pockets is blown apart holding the door. *"Tell Hettie... her stitching... was terrible."*

### Act III: The Last Word (levels 38–50)

11. **Hettie's Folly.** A clue inside Mr. Pockets' remains points to Hettie's first workshop, a floating junkyard of
    failed prototypes that still move. At its heart, in a chair, in goggles: Hettie Bellweather, alive. *"Took you long
    enough."* She faked her death to draw Vandersloot into the open, because he'd learned the Last Word was real. She
    built it in a fit of guilt fifty years ago and has never been able to decide whether the world deserved it, so she
    needed an heir who'd decide better. She watched the whole hunt through Mr. Pockets' glass eye. Sunny is furious:
    *"You owe me six years of chicken feed and a funeral."* Two more Masterworks wait in the Folly's junk.
12. **Vandersloot City.** Take the Masterworks back from the factory island, with the Auditor and a turncoat Prudence
    alongside. The fireworks plant goes up spectacularly. Colonel Catherine Wheel commands the Fireworks Division, and
    the Rival is in Vandersloot's trophy vault.
13. **The Expo.** Read together, the engravings on the stolen Masterworks give the Last Word's hiding place, and
    Vandersloot read them first. He unveils it at the Grand Gun Exposition, to an audience of every gunmaker in the sky. The final battle runs through halls of every gun ever made. Encore was in
    a display case the whole time. Vandersloot climbs into the **Mk. Infinity**, a walking suit built from a thousand of
    his own cheap guns. Hettie fights beside Sunny in a prototype of her own. It explodes, as her prototypes do.

### Ending

Sunny ends up holding the Last Word. Fire it at Vandersloot's factories and his empire of cheap guns jams forever.
Lock it away, as Hettie did. Or fire it straight up, and every gun in the hall jams, Hettie's and Sunny's included
(Rosa has a crate of spares). Hettie laughs until she cries: *"Well. That's one way to retire."*

Vandersloot is arrested by the Auditor for tax fraud, which hurts him more than any bullet. Hettie names Sunny the head
of Bellweather Arms. Sunny's first act: rebuild the barn and give Mr. Pockets a new body, with better stitching.

## Side quests (samples)

- **Husband Number Four.** A poet wants his love letters back from one of the trial vaults. They're awful. Read them
  aloud for a bonus.
- **The Calibre Family Business.** Big Mama's boys want to go straight. Help them open a legitimate shooting gallery,
  and defend it on opening night.
- **Chicken Run.** Hettie's chickens escaped into the County Fair. One of them is armed.
- **Spark's Horses.** Doc Spark's clockwork horses are loose in a frontier town and won't stop galloping.

## Loot in this world

- **Who makes the guns.** Every maker in the world section, all on Hettie's standard, which is the parts system in the
  fiction. Vandersloot is the grey common in every crate; Bellweather parts are the prize.
- **Rarity and the beams.** Rarity is craftsmanship. Every gun built to the Bellweather Standard carries a tiny **finder
  flare** (Hettie hated losing good guns) that pops when it hits the ground, colored by grade. That's the loot beam.
- **Chests.** Supply Crates are Vandersloot shipping crates with his face stenciled on them. Strongboxes are bank and
  estate safes. Reliquaries are Hettie's trial vaults, built in granite and gold "for drama."
- **Elements (when they come).** **Fireworks** (explosive and colorful), Shock, **Sticky** (preserves) and Corrosive
  (Big Mama's moonshine).

## The game's systems, explained

| System | In this story |
|---|---|
| Death and respawn | **Second Chance Stations**, Hettie's patented booths: *"A Bellweather product. Results not guaranteed."* |
| Falling off an island | Hettie's Patented Pocket Parachute pops out and a little propeller flies you back |
| The HUD and gun stats | Hettie's appraiser goggles: seeing a gun's numbers is her invention |
| Loot beams | Finder flares |
| Bestiary | *Mr. Pockets' Opinions*, snide notes on everything, with Hettie's corrections in the margins |
| Level-ups | Fame: every level is a newspaper headline about you |

## Endgame (levels 50–70)

- **The Workshop.** Craft your own Masterworks at Skyreach from Bellweather parts. This builds straight on the existing
  parts system.
- **Proving Grounds.** Hettie's arena challenges with rotating modifiers.
- **The Gun Show.** A traveling event circuit with a new rule set every week.
- **Hettie's Hard Mode.** Replay the trials at higher tiers for better rolls.
- **Franchise Raids.** Vandersloot's remaining factory islands, still producing without him.

## Production notes

- **Loot is the story:** the main quest is legendary weapons, so the parts system becomes the showcase and crafting
  extends what's already built.
- **Compact levels:** each trial is a small self-contained level, cheap to build and easy to keep on budget.
- **Comedy needs writing and timing.** Hettie's recordings and Mr. Pockets carry it. They can be text first and voiced
  later.
- **Risk:** low to medium. The main risk is tone, not technology.

## Why pick it, and what to watch

Pick it if you want the loot to *be* the story: twelve legendaries with personalities, compact trial levels, a cast
with huge character, and the best fit for the parts system already built. Watch out: it reads as "Borderlands with a
cowboy hat" unless the heart (Hettie, Sunny, Mr. Pockets) is real. Jokes age fast. And your choices so far (stylized
realism, a realistic wolf spider, walnut-and-steel guns) lean grounded, so this is the furthest from that.
