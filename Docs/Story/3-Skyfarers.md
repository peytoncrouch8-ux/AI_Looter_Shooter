# 3. Skyfarers

> *Half a map to the greatest treasure in the sky. Every crew alive wants the other half. The X is closer than anyone
> thinks.*

A dying sky-pirate crashes in Skyreach's orchard with half a map to the lost Hoard of the Calico Queen. Tam Reed, a
farmhand who has never left the island, signs on with the charming Captain Margo Sable to find it. They race the
Admiralty, a pirate king and, in the end, Margo herself, back to the one place no one thought to look.

## Cold open

Thirty years ago, a sky battle at sunset: a fleet of black-and-gold ships against the white sails of the Admiralty.
The flagship of the legendary **Calico Queen** burns, banks into a towering cloud and is gone. Cannon smoke drifts over
empty sky. Cut to today, on Skyreach: a sleepy farm in the afternoon. An old woman shells peas on the porch, humming a
sea shanty under her breath. *"Tam! The fence won't fix itself!"*

## The world

- **The open sky.** Free islands drift on the trade winds in slow circuits, and anything that floats can be sailed:
  airships ride lodestone ballast and canvas. Where there's trade there are pirates.
- **The Meridian Admiralty** rules the wealthy inner islands and claims the whole sky, but can't hold the outer drift.
  Its dream is **the Meridian**: every island chained into a fixed lattice under navy guns.
- **The Calico Queen.** Thirty years ago Captain Isadora Drake ruled the outer sky with a fleet of black-and-gold ships.
  When the Admiralty finally cornered her, her flagship flew into a cloud bank and was never seen again, and with it
  went **the Calico Hoard**: the plunder of a hundred ships and something called the Heart of the Sky. The map to it
  was split in two and given to the two people she trusted most: her quartermaster, and the cabin girl she was raising
  as her own.
- **The secret.** The Calico Queen's flagship was never an ordinary ship. It was **the Lantern**, a vessel the size of
  an island, built by the vanished Keepers who raised the sky, with a **Helm** that can steer any island, not just
  itself. After her last battle, Isadora hid it in plain sight: she let it drift to the edge of the sky, let soil and
  grass grow over its decks, built a farm on top, and retired. The Lantern is Skyreach. The windmill is its mast, the
  ruined lookout is its helm tower, and Granny Nell Thistlewood is the Calico Queen.

## Protagonist: Tam Reed

Found as a baby in a lifeboat drifting past Skyreach and raised by Granny Nell on the Thistlewood farm, Tam is a
quick, curious, chore-dodging twenty-year-old who has spent every evening of their life on the ruined lookout,
watching airships pass and wishing. Tam can shoot, because Granny insisted, and has no idea why Granny shoots like
that.

The arc runs from wanting to leave home to understanding what home was, and from deckhand to first mate to captain.
Tam learns that people are more than the worst thing they did, and less than the legends about them.

> *"I've wanted to see the world my whole life. Nobody mentioned the world shoots back."*

## Antagonist: Grand Admiral Severin Holt

Cold, precise and incorruptible, Holt commands the Meridian Admiralty's fleet from the dreadnought *Inexorable*. When
he was nine, the Red Wake pirates burned his home island while he hid in a flour barrel. Everything since has been in
service of one idea: a sky where nothing drifts without a flag and no child is ever raided again. He wants the
Lantern's Helm to chain every free island into the Meridian at once. He isn't cruel. He just doesn't believe in
freedom, because he's seen what it costs.

> *"Order is a mercy, child. The sky has simply never been shown any."*

### Captain Margo Sable

The other face of the antagonist. Margo is the captain of the *Sweet Persistence*: charming, scarred, funny,
generous, a brilliant sailor and a better liar. She rescues Tam, teaches them the sky and becomes the mentor they
always wanted. She was also Isadora Drake's cabin girl, nearly a daughter to her, captured and left behind when the
Calico fleet fell, and she spent eleven years on an Admiralty prison barge believing her captain had abandoned her. She holds the other half of
the map, and she'll sell anyone, Tam included, to finally get what she's owed. Then she'll regret it.

> *"Rule one, sweet thing: never trust a pirate. Rule two: I'm the exception. Rule three: see rule one."*

## Enemies and creatures

- **The Red Wake**: Crale's pirates. Deckhands with cutlasses and pistols, Powder Monkeys with grenades,
  Swingers who come in on ropes, and Brutes with Thunderdrum shotguns.
- **Admiralty Marines**: disciplined riflemen in white and blue, Shield Lines, Marksmen, Grenadiers, Boarding Parties,
  and **Officers** whose death breaks the squad's morale.
- **Wreckers**: scavenger clans of the Brass Cemetery and the Shoals who wear salvaged ship armor.
- **Creatures**:
  - Meadow Wolf spiders on Skyreach.
  - Sky Jumpers in the Shoals' wrecks and rigging.
  - Cliff Huntsmen on Gallowglass's walls.
  - MossBacks in the Shoals' sky-kelp forests.
  - Ember Tarantulas in the Brass Cemetery's burning hulks.
  - **Sky Rays** (gliding mantas), **Storm Eels** in the Tempest Wall, and **Old Knotty**, a sky-kraken the size of a
    frigate living in the Shoals' wrecks.
- **Keeper guardians**: brass-and-stone wardens deep in the Lantern's hull that wake up confused in Act III.

## Skyreach in this story

1. **Move.** An ordinary day on the Thistlewood farm. Granny sends Tam to the village.
2. **Follow the road to the village.** The Crossing, where everyone seems oddly fond of sea shanties for people who
   live in a meadow.
3. **The gun rack.** The barkeep of the Crossed Keys keeps a rifle on the rack outside "for varmints." He hands it over
   with a wink.
4. **The dummies.** Granny's scarecrows in the meadow, which she shoots for practice from the porch, a hundred meters
   away, without looking up from her peas.
5. **The spiders.** The woods past the pond.
6. **The loadout.** Bash would be impressed later. For now, Tam checks the rifle.
7. **The lookout.** Tam's favorite spot. A burning skiff limps across the sky, chased by a black-sailed airship, and
   crashes in the orchard below.

## Plot

### Act I: The Halfmap (levels 1–15)

1. The tutorial, ending with the crash. In the orchard, a dying old pirate presses an etched brass half-map, a
   battered compass and a clockwork parrot into Tam's hands: *"Find the Queen. Tell her Margo's coming. Tell her the
   Lantern's waking."*
2. Crale's *Gallows Bird* lands raiders to take the map. Tam and Granny defend the farmhouse, and Granny shoots like
   nobody Tam has ever seen.
3. The *Sweet Persistence* swoops out of the clouds and blasts the *Gallows Bird*. Captain Margo Sable charms
   everyone, admires the map, and offers Tam a berth: *"That map's worth a fortune, and you're worth more alive."*
   Granny goes very still when she sees Margo's face, says nothing, and lets Tam go: *"Keep your powder dry, and your captain closer."* Admiral
   Feathers squawks *"Captain!"* as Granny speaks. Tam assumes he means Margo.
4. **Port Gallant.** The legend of the Calico Queen in every tavern; Bash signs on. The half-map's marks point to the
   Queen's old haunts. A brawl with the Red Wake ends with Cleaver Moll.
5. **The Shoals.** Diving the wrecks of the Calico decoy fleet. A logbook says the Admiralty took a brass key off the
   Queen's ship, the **Calico Key**, and keeps it as a trophy at Gallowglass. Old Knotty has made the decoy fleet her
   nest.
6. Crale ambushes the *Sweet Persistence* in a cloud bank. A boarding fight across two decks; Crale escapes.

### Act II: The Tempest (levels 15–40)

7. **Gallowglass Fort.** Infiltrate the Admiralty fortress, quietly or not, and steal the Calico Key from the trophy
   hall. Commander Varga duels Tam on the battlements. Beaten, she lets them go. She has her own opinion of Holt.
8. **The Brass Cemetery.** The Calico crew's graves, the scavenger clans and Crale's last stand aboard a crashed
   galleon. Dying, he sneers: *"Ask your captain who she was before. Ask her about the Queen's cabin girl."*
9. Margo tells Tam the truth, or most of it: the cabin girl, the capture, eleven years on a prison barge, the Queen
   who never came back for her. She shows Tam the other half of the map. Together the halves chart a course through
   the Tempest Wall, then trail off into a blank: the last mark only shows under the Calico Key's lens. Tam trusts her.
10. **The Tempest Wall.** Storm islands, lightning, the eye of the storm, and the Admiralty fleet waiting in it.
    Margo has traded Tam, the map and the Key for a full pardon and a share. The crew is captured. Holt is courteous
    about it.
11. **The *Penance*.** A prison break. Admiral Feathers steals the cell keys. On the way out, in Holt's cabin, the
    joined map lies under the Calico Key's lens, and the X is Skyreach. Feathers takes the Key too, and the crew
    escapes on a stolen launch. Doc Pembrook goes pale: thirty years ago he watched the Queen's flagship fly into a
    cloud and come out an island. Then he hums the Calico Queen's battle shanty, the one Granny hums over her peas, and
    Tam knows.

### Act III: The Lantern (levels 40–50)

12. **Skyreach besieged.** Holt's fleet got there first. Marines are camped in the Crossing. Then the villagers pull
    cutlasses and blunderbusses out from under their floorboards: the sheriff, the blacksmith, the barkeep, the whole
    retired Calico crew. Granny Nell walks out of the farmhouse in a black-and-gold coat: *"Name's Isadora Drake. Get
    off my farm."*
13. **The Hold.** Tam and Isadora go down through the farmhouse cellar into the Lantern's ancient hull, past Keeper
    guardians waking from thirty years' sleep, to the Helm. The Calico Key turns.
14. **The Lantern wakes.** The windmill's tower rises and its blades unfold into great sails. The ruined lookout lifts
    into a helm tower. The island tears loose from its drift and sails into the Admiralty fleet.
15. Aboard the *Inexorable*, Margo watches the island come, and spikes the dreadnought's main guns before they can
    fire.
16. **The *Inexorable*.** The crew boards the dreadnought and fights deck by deck to the bridge, then down to the
    stolen Keeper engine Holt means to ram the Lantern with. Holt fights like he commands: patient, exact,
    surrounded by his best.

### Ending

The *Inexorable* breaks and falls. Tam can pull Holt from the wreck to stand trial, or leave him to his ship. With the
Helm, Tam does what Isadora never dared: one turn of the wheel and the Meridian's chains snap, and every island the
Admiralty held drifts free. Then Tam locks the Helm's hold on other islands for good.

On the deck, Isadora and Margo face each other as themselves for the first time in thirty years. *"For thirty years I
thought the Admiralty hanged you, Margo. If I'd known, I'd have torn the sky apart."* Margo laughs, cries a little, and takes exactly one small chest from
the Hoard before sailing off with a salute. *"She always did take the small chest,"* Isadora says. Then she hands Tam
the wheel. *"She's yours now. Try not to sink her."*

## Side quests (samples)

- **The Lost Verses.** Each verse of the Calico Queen's shanty hides a clue to one of her caches. Collect them from
  old pirates across the sky.
- **Parrot Business.** A collector of clockwork birds steals Admiral Feathers. Get him back before he's taken apart.
- **One Last Job.** In Act III, each retired pirate in the Crossing has a heist they never finished.
- **The Regatta.** A race through the Shoals with a legendary as the prize and Old Knotty's children in the way.

## Loot in this world

- **Who makes the guns.** Every navy, colony and smithy in the sky, rebuilt by pirates from parts. Coachman and Breacher
  are Admiralty issue, Kestrel bullpups are airship crews' favorites, Scrapjack builds from wrecks, Thunderdrum makes
  deck guns, and Zephyr is Port Gallant's flashy custom shop.
- **Rarity and the beams.** Rarity is provenance: deckhand junk, colonial issue, navy issue, officer's custom and named
  legends. Jory's **Calico Compass** points at treasure; its needle throws the loot beam, colored by worth.
- **Chests.** Supply Crates are Admiralty cargo. Strongboxes are merchant safes, the point of every convoy raid.
  Reliquaries are Keeper vaults where the Calico Queen hid her best guns.
- **Elements (when they come).** Powder (explosive), Storm (shock), Frost (the thin, cold air up high) and Gale
  (knockback, best used next to an edge).

## The game's systems, explained

| System | In this story |
|---|---|
| Falling off an island | Admiral Feathers dives after you with a line and the crew hauls you back |
| Death and respawn | The crew drags you aboard: respawn at the ship or the last dock |
| The HUD | The glass of the Calico Compass |
| Loot beams | The compass needle marking treasure |
| Bestiary | *The Almanac of the Open Sky*, an old pirate's field guide signed only "I.D." The handwriting matches Granny's recipe cards. Tam doesn't notice until Act II |
| Level-ups | The price on Tam's head; every level is a new wanted poster |

## Endgame (levels 50–70)

- **Sail Skyreach.** The island is now a ship and the hub moves. Pick a heading on the sky chart.
- **Treasure Maps.** Treasure islands assembled from kits, each with a riddle, a dig site and guardians, in tiers.
- **Convoys.** Raid armored Admiralty and merchant convoys for Strongbox payrolls.
- **Pirate Lords.** With Crale gone, new pirate kings rise to claim the Red Wake. Rotating named bosses.
- **Storm Chasing.** The deepest islands inside the Tempest Wall, at the top tier.

## Production notes

- **Reuses:** the tutorial island becomes the final act's battlefield and the endgame's hub: the biggest payoff any
  story gives the existing level. The windmill becomes the mast and the lookout the helm.
- **New:** airship decks as small levels (the *Sweet Persistence*'s deck is the hub for Acts I and II); scripted
  boarding sequences; the island-sails sequence (new windmill parts, wind, a sky dome and clouds that scroll so the
  island seems to move while the level stays put); storm effects, priced on Medium.
- **Keep ships as set pieces** you fight on, not vehicles you steer. Steering is a different game and a big bill.
- **Risk:** medium to high, mostly from airship content.

## Why pick it, and what to watch

Pick it for the most fun and the widest appeal of the eight, a crew that becomes a family, and the best payoff for
the island already built: Skyreach turns out to be the treasure and then becomes your ship. Watch out: airship content
is expensive, so keep the ship scenes as set pieces. The lighter tone must stay grimy (pirates are dirty), so it
matches "rustic, harsh, lived-in." And the Treasure Island structure is familiar, so the Granny reveal has to land.
Seed it from the first scene.
