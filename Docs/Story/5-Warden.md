# 5. Warden

> *The frontier is a museum. Its owners are waking up. And you were built to guard the door.*

Ward has worked the Bellamy farm on Skyreach for three years, ever since Joel Bellamy pulled them out of the creek with
no memory. The day a scarecrow on the range calls Ward by a serial number, the frontier cracks open. The islands are
machines, the frontier life was designed, and the people who built it all are waking up to take it back.

## Cold open

White text types itself onto black, like a terminal: `HERITAGE HABITAT 114 — SKYREACH. POPULATION 61. WARDEN
ASSIGNED: NONE.` A pause. `UNIT W-7: DECOMMISSIONED.` Cut to a body falling past clouds, hitting a pond and drifting
down a creek toward the edge of the world. Joel Bellamy, fishing, drops his rod and hauls them out a few meters short
of the falls. *"Hattie! HATTIE! There's a person in the creek!"*

## The world

- **Four hundred years ago,** the surface was buried by **the Grey**, a self-replicating dust that choked everything
  below a certain height. The **Halvorsen-Adair Lift Consortium** built the floating islands as refuges, each held up
  by a **lift engine** around a glowing orange **lattice-core**.
- **The Sleepers.** The Consortium's founders and investors, forty thousand of the richest people alive, went into
  cryogenic vaults to wait out the Grey: *we'll wake when the world is clean.*
- **The Heritage Program.** To keep the islands running, the Consortium settled them with caretakers, workers and
  their families, in **Heritage Habitats**: deliberately simple frontier lives, so the caretakers would be
  self-sufficient, wouldn't tamper with the machines and wouldn't need much. Their descendants are today's frontier
  folk. They know nothing. Their laws, their almanacs and the **Charter** read in church every Sunday were written by
  the Consortium.
- **The Wardens** are synthetic guardians built to look and live human. They maintain the engines, keep the peace and
  protect the vaults. Most were shut down over the centuries. A few never were, and the best known is **the
  Marshal**, the legendary lawman of the Reaches. Folk think "the Marshal" is a title passed down. It's the same man, four hundred years old.
- **The secret.** The Grey burned itself out a hundred and fifty years ago. The surface is green again. The
  Consortium's systems saw it and started the **Dawn Protocol** to wake the Sleepers, and the Marshal has been holding
  it back ever since. Now it has started on its own.
- What folks call Keeper relics are Consortium hardware: Reliquaries are supply vaults, their orange "runes" are
  interface text, and the crystal floating over the lid is a lattice-core.

## Protagonist: Ward

The Bellamys named them Ward, as in a ward of the family. Hattie thought it was sweet. Ward has worked the farm for
three years: eats, sleeps, bleeds, burns the biscuits, and is good with tools in a way nobody can explain. Clem, the
Bellamys' twelve-year-old, thinks Ward is the most interesting person alive.

Ward is **W-7**, a Warden. Three years ago they found out the truth, that the Sleepers were mostly dead and the surface
was green, and wanted to tell the folk. The Marshal shot them and threw them off the Anchor. Ward fell a very long way
and landed in Skyreach's pond, their memory gone.

The arc: *am I a person?* becomes *I chose these people, and that was real.* Ward was built to serve the Sleepers and
chooses the farmers. Gameplay grows with the identity: each restored memory is a new ability.

> *"I bleed, I sleep, I burn the biscuits. If that's a machine, it's a very convincing one."*

## Antagonist: the Marshal

Marshal Josiah Gentry, **Warden Prime**: duster, silver star, a grey mustache, kind eyes. He has kept the peace in the
Reaches for four hundred years. He remembers every person he has buried, and he has buried generations. Folk tell
stories about him the way people tell stories about saints. He is good at his job, and he loves the frontier.

He believes in the Charter: the islands belong to the Sleepers, and the folk are their caretakers. He held back the
Dawn Protocol for a hundred and fifty years because he was afraid of what the Sleepers would do to "his people." Now
his own core is failing and the Protocol has started without him. So he'll hand the islands over in good order, and
that means quietly removing every settlement that has learned too much, so the Sleepers wake to find farmers instead of
rebels. *If they wake to rebels, they'll purge everyone.* It's protection, by his lights, and it's murder.

> *"Now, I've buried more folks than you've ever met, and I loved every one of them. Don't you tell me about mercy."*

### Chairwoman Lucienne Adair

The founder of the Consortium and the first Sleeper to wake. Brilliant, charming and monstrous in her sense of what
she's owed, she sees the frontier folk as staff whose contract has expired. Her plan is to "consolidate" the
islands, sinking the ones she doesn't need and sending their people down to the surface as labor to build her new
cities.

The thing she won't say: of forty thousand Sleepers, three hundred survived. The vaults failed a century ago. Her
nation is a ghost, but whoever holds the vault holds every machine in the sky.

> *"Thank you for your service. Your employment is concluded."*

## Enemies and creatures

- **Deputies**, the Marshal's synthetic lawmen: dusters, hats and smooth porcelain faces. Riflemen, fast Riders,
  **Hangmen** (heavies with chain lassos), Sharpshooters, and **Undertakers** that rebuild fallen Deputies (kill them
  first).
- **Groundskeepers** and **Tenders**: maintenance machines (mowers, pruners, eight-legged engine crawlers) turned
  hostile by the Protocol.
- **Sentinels**: flying drones the folk have always called "hawks."
- **The Faithful**: human zealots of the Marshal's church.
- **Scrappers**: salvage raiders, hostile until Copperline.
- **Elysium's household**: elegant white-and-gold Valets and heavy security frames.
- **Creatures**: Meadow Wolf spiders nesting in warm engine vents, MossBacks on the damp hydroponic undersides, Cliff
  Huntsmen on engine housings, Ember Tarantulas around overheating cores, Sky Jumpers on the antennas. On the surface,
  in the post-game: **Greylings**, animals that survived the Grey and were changed by it.

## Skyreach in this story

The tutorial plays as written, with one change: the HUD isn't there at the start. It switches on piece by piece after
the range, as Ward's systems wake.

1. **Move.** A normal morning on the Bellamy farm.
2. **Follow the road to the village.** Errands for Hattie.
3. **The gun rack.** The village rifle, for varmints.
4. **The dummies.** The scarecrows on the range are old Consortium calibration posts the villagers dressed up. After
   Ward's last shot, one of them flickers and speaks: *"Unit W-7. Calibration complete. Welcome back, Warden."* The HUD
   boots up. Clem saw everything.
5. **The spiders.** The spiders in the grove are nesting around warm air rising from the ground. Under the roots: a
   metal hatch with orange writing on it.
6. **The loadout.** Ward checks the rifle and sees its stats, which no farmhand should be able to.
7. **The lookout.** At dusk, every island in sight fires a beam of light into the sky at once: every Reliquary waking.
   The Dawn Protocol has begun. That night, Deputies come to the Bellamy farm asking about "a stray unit." Hattie lies
   to their faces. Their faces don't move.

## Plot

### Act I: Heritage (levels 1–15)

1. The tutorial, the voice on the range, the beacons, the Deputies at the door.
2. **Fairhaven.** Ward goes to the Marshal, because he's the law. He's kind. He pours coffee, listens, promises to look
   into it. That night his Deputies come to collect Ward. A chase through town, the Faithful shouting "demon," and a
   Hangman in the street.
3. **The Undercroft.** Professor Fitch has known about the hatch for years and never dared open it. He and Ward go
   down. Under the farm are miles of humming machinery, and Ward knows the way. The Caretaker, an old engine mech gone
   wrong, guards the core.
4. **The record.** At a terminal, Ward's own file: *W-7. Assignment: Heritage oversight. Status: decommissioned by
   Warden Prime.* Ward comes home in pieces. Clem sits beside them on the porch: *"You still burn the biscuits. You're
   still you."*

### Act II: The Dawn Protocol (levels 15–42)

5. **Copperline.** Ward needs parts and allies. The Scrappers want Ward *for* parts, until a Deputy purge hits their
   island and Ward stands with them. Del Hart joins.
6. **The Signal.** The Faithful's cathedral is the relay the Marshal uses to send the Charter sermons to every pulpit.
   Del wants to tell everyone the truth; Fitch fears the panic. Ward broadcasts. Some islands riot, some rejoice, and
   the Marshal answers with "culls." Reverend Cole defends his saint's tower to the end.
7. **The Foundry.** Shut down Deputy production. On the line, Ward wakes **Dime**, a Deputy who doesn't know what it is
   either. The Foreman runs the factory floor.
8. **Memory.** Ward's past comes back: the Marshal's protégé, the discovery, the shot on the Anchor.
9. **The Anchor.** The Courthouse at noon. The square empties. The Marshal walks out to meet Ward in the street: a
   real showdown, then a long fight when his skin splits and the old frame underneath keeps coming. Dying, he holds out
   his star, the master key to everything: *"I kept them safe four hundred years by keeping them small. Maybe you'll do
   better."*
10. The Dawn Protocol completes. Lucienne Adair opens her eyes. The other survivors stay in their pods, waiting on her
    order.

### Act III: Elysium (levels 42–50)

11. Lucienne's voice comes out of every pulpit, every Reliquary and every Deputy at once: *"Thank you for your service.
    Your employment is concluded."* Her Valets seize the Anchor. Island by island, she begins sinking the habitats she
    calls redundant, sending their people down.
12. **Elysium.** Ward breaks into the vault under the Anchor: silent white halls, gardens under a false sun, a cryo hall
    of forty thousand pods, almost all of them dark. The Concierge politely tries to kill Ward in every room.
13. **The Chairwoman.** Lucienne meets Ward in the Executive Frame, a white-and-gold war chassis built to protect the
    founders.

### Ending

With the Marshal's star, Ward holds the vault. They can wake the three hundred survivors as citizens with no power
over anyone (*"You'll farm like everyone else"*), or leave them sleeping forever. Then Ward opens the **Descent**: the
old lift shafts to the surface, sealed for four hundred years. The last scene is the folk of Skyreach seeing the green
world below for the first time, and Clem at the edge, grinning at Ward: *"Race you down."*

## Side quests (samples)

- **Biscuits.** Hattie teaches Ward to bake, and Ward finds out their taste sensors were never calibrated.
- **The Good Deputy.** A Deputy has decided it really is a sheriff and is protecting a town from raiders. Decide its
  fate.
- **Fitch's Museum.** Recover pieces of the old world for the Professor: a phone, a toaster, a photograph of a city.
  Each comes with a story.
- **The Squad.** Find the cores of Ward's old Warden squad. Each holds a memory, and one of them is still awake.

## Loot in this world

- **Who makes the guns.** Frontier gunsmiths have built the same Consortium blueprints for four hundred years:
  Homestead, Regulator, Coachman and Breacher are all Heritage designs, and Kestrel bullpups were the Wardens' own
  issue. That's why every part fits every gun: they were standardized on purpose. Consortium weapons, with orange lattice accents, are the top tiers.
- **Rarity and the beams.** Every Consortium-made part carries a recovery beacon. When a gun is dropped it pings the
  network, and Ward sees the ping as a beam, colored by **clearance level**, which is the rarity.
- **Chests.** Supply Crates are Consortium caches with stenciled serials. Strongboxes are Heritage bank safes.
  Reliquaries are Consortium vaults that open for a Warden's access.
- **Elements (when they come).** **Arc** (shock, disables machines), **Thermal** (fire), **Grey** (nanodust that eats
  armor) and **Lattice** (orange lift: targets float).

## The game's systems, explained

This is the story where every system gets an in-world reason:

| System | In this story |
|---|---|
| The HUD | Ward's own interface: it switches on in the tutorial, and its dark glass and cyan lines are how a Warden sees |
| Death and respawn | Ward is rebuilt at the nearest **Cradle**, a Consortium repair station |
| Falling off an island | Ward's emergency lift field returns them to the last stable ground |
| Level-ups | Restored firmware. At milestone levels a memory comes back, so the story drips through progression |
| Bestiary | Ward's threat database: Consortium classifications with Ward's own notes underneath |
| Loot beams and rarity | Recovery beacons, colored by clearance |
| Shared ammo | Heritage cartridges are standard across every maker |

## Endgame (levels 50–70)

- **The Surface.** Expeditions into the green old world: overgrown cities, Greylings, rogue Consortium machines. Claim
  outposts for the settlers coming down: a new frontier, literally.
- **Recall Orders.** Bounties on rogue Deputies and Wardens, named synthetics built from rolled parts. They drop their
  parts.
- **Vault Protocols.** Consortium security floors at rising **Clearance** tiers (the difficulty).
- **Overclock.** Difficulty modifiers presented as Ward's firmware settings.

- The scarecrow that says *"Welcome back, Warden,"* and the HUD booting up.
- Every island's beacon firing into the dusk at once.
- Walking under the farm through miles of glowing machinery.
- The noon showdown with the Marshal in the courthouse square.

## Production notes

- **A second art kit:** Consortium tech (clean worn metal, glass, orange holograms), kept textured and in the
  stylized-realism style so it sits next to the farm. The Reliquary's granite, gold and orange runes already point
  that way.
- **Machines are cheap to animate:** stiff, mechanical motion is forgiving and suits the procedural, code-driven
  approach that already moves the spider.
- **The diegetic HUD** is a UI design opportunity: the Concept C dark glass and cyan lines already look like a
  machine's interface. The tutorial can hide HUD pieces until the range scene.
- **Risk:** medium to high, from the second art kit. The twist has to be seeded from the first minutes.

## Why pick it, and what to watch

Pick it if you want every mechanic (HUD, respawn, levels, loot beams, even the shared ammo) to have a reason inside the
fiction, a twist that turns the rustic tutorial into a mystery, and machine enemies that multiply variety cheaply.
Watch out: *Westworld* and Horizon Zero Dawn are the obvious comparisons. The frontier folk here are real people and
the robot is the hero, so lean on that. Two art kits cost more than one.
